\subsection{Data and local tracking}
We use the released per-vehicle, no-fusion detections from DMSTrack
\cite{chiu2024dmstrack} on V2V4Real~\cite{xu2023v2v4real}. Nine validation
sequences provide the development subset; the main evaluation contains
25 complete train sequences.
The two subsets contain 1,993 and 5,601 paired frames. All 34 sequences
informed method development, and the released detector was trained on
the train split; the evaluation therefore measures fusion performance on
this development corpus.

We track planar car centers within 40 m of either sensor and the
annotated ego-frame rectangle
$[-70.4,70.4]\times[-40,40]$ m, excluding a 3 m disk around each vehicle.
The same domain applies to truth and estimates. Each local sensor uses
its own 40 m support, with transforms supplied by the dataset.
Detections retain the released confidence cutoff of 0.20.
The model uses current ego coordinates without ego-motion compensation.

All methods share the score-aware LBP-LMB local update. A constant-velocity
model uses a 0.1 s interval, survival probability 0.99, acceleration
intensity 25 m$^2$/s$^3$, position covariance $I_2$ m$^2$, and in-domain
$p_D=0.9$. Poisson clutter has mean three per sensor scan and spatial
density $3/(\pi40^2)$. Each previous-scan detection creates an independent
local birth with existence 0.01, zero velocity, and covariance
$\operatorname{diag}(16,16,225,225)$. Tracks with $r\leq0.001$ are pruned;
retained mixtures are moment-projected to one Gaussian, and MAP
cardinality determines set extraction. History qualification and absent-label
censoring are shared across the geometric methods. A censor uses existence
0.001 when the represented mean lies in its sensor's support.

\subsection{Detection-score likelihood}
Following post-hoc probability calibration~\cite{niculescu2005calibration},
we map detector scores to probabilities with a monotone logistic fit.
Per-sensor one-to-one assignment within 12 m supplies offline calibration
labels. We fit $u(s)=\sigma(a\,\operatorname{logit}s+b)$, $a\geq0$, by
sequence-balanced binary log loss with regularization $0.001a^2$.
Development sequences use leave-one-sequence-out fits. The main evaluation
uses a fit on all nine development sequences, held fixed at
$a=2.8858$, $b=2.1564$.
The score enters each local association likelihood once through
\begin{equation}
 L(s)=\frac{u(s)}{1-u(s)}\,\frac{1-\pi}{\pi},
 \label{eq:mark}
\end{equation}
where $\pi$ is the sequence-balanced fitting prevalence. This separable
mark model is common to GCE and every main baseline. Online tracking and
fusion use detections and communicated beliefs.

\subsection{Communication and baselines}
Reliable links deliver both directed packets each scan. Intermittent
links combine independent directed loss of 0.1 with a bidirectional
outage over the middle 20\% of scans. Methods share the delivery trace
and one packet opportunity per direction per scan. A receiver pools its
own and a delivered neighbor's update with weights $[0.5,0.5]$; without
a neighbor packet it keeps its local update. We account for 16,384-byte
fragments and a 256-byte control charge per scan. Delivery is independent
of packet length. GCE uses $\rho=0.25$ and $\lambda=5$ s.

\emph{No-age KLA} uses qualified weights $\alpha,b$.
\emph{Recency} replaces $b$ with $q$ in existence, while
\emph{Conservative} uses $\beta$ without a current correction.
\emph{Capped-score} and \emph{Capped-cal.} bound recency existence by
$\min(r_{\rm rec},\max(r_{\rm noage},U))$: $U$ is the largest
association-weighted raw or calibrated score among currently observed,
upweighted sources with existence at least 0.5.
\emph{Scalar} uses GCE's base and admission gates to correct existence
log-odds, retaining the original spatial pool and normalizer without a
Gaussian ratio or curvature test.

\emph{MIL-AM} adapts the public manuscript's augmented label assignment
and common/exclusive subspace fusion~\cite{gao2019milpreprint}.
Symmetric KL cost with unmatched cost 50 per side aligns Gaussian labels;
the geometric methods use the same alignment. Common labels use
arithmetic existence pooling and existence-weighted spatial mixtures;
exclusive labels retain their source density.
\emph{TC-5/TC-10} use the author's kinematic matching and two-stage
multi-neighbor functions~\cite{nguyen2021tc}, with five- or ten-scan
histories, matching order one, and cutoff 12 m. They combine independently
labeled local tracks and retain the architecture's separate local
filters. \emph{Local} uses the shared backend without communication.

\subsection{Metrics and ablations}
We report position OSPA with $p=2,c=12$ m
\cite{schuhmacher2008ospa} and the missed and false squared-cost
components of GOSPA with $p=2,c=12$ m,$\alpha=2$~\cite{gospa2017}.
Common-target RMSE pools errors on robot--time--truth instances matched
by both compared methods. Sequences receive equal weight; 95\%
percentile intervals use 10,000 paired resamples of complete sequences.

Three ablations remove the curvature guard, conservative history switch,
or positive score constraint, one at a time, and run the complete
recursive tracker. The score ablation retains the common local score
model. Scalar provides a separate reference for existence-only
correction.

\subsection{Mechanism and outage diagnostics}
To distinguish immediate fusion effects from recursive changes, we also
hold every GCE input, label match, base weight, and admitted increment
fixed. Write $z=\sum_j\beta_jz_j+\sum_j\bar\kappa_j\delta_j$.
At each delivered receiver-scan, a $2\times2$ substitution uses the base
or corrected spatial mean and either $z+\log I_0$ or $z+\log I$ for
existence, where $I_0$ is the base spatial integral. All cells use the
same pruning, MAP cardinality, and output domain. These substitutions
are evaluated once on GCE-visited inputs; the original GCE output
continues the recursion. The base/base cell is thus a guarded scalar
substitution, distinct from the Scalar trajectory in Table~\ref{tab:main}.

We partition each sequence at $\lfloor0.4T\rfloor$ and
$\lfloor0.6T\rfloor$, matching the imposed outage, and compare OSPA
before, during, and after that interval. Reliable links use the same
scene-time windows. Each phase first averages both receivers and all
included scans, then weights sequences equally. All phases and all four
substitution cells are retained; their paired sequence intervals are
descriptive and unadjusted for multiple comparisons.

Analytical checks cover shared-prior fusion, conflicting
observations, zero gates, single-source updates, and nonintegrable
aggregates. Codec evaluation uses all 34 sequences under both link
conditions and compares decoded values and tracking trajectories.

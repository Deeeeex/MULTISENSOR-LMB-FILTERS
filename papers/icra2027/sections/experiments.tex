\section{Experimental Protocol}
\subsection{Prescribed mobile sensing scenarios}
Eight simulated robots follow two smooth patrol families for 120 frames
at $\Delta t=0.5$ s. Their maximum speed is below $3$ m/s and acceleration
below $2.1$ m/s$^2$. Radio range is $24$ m and circular sensing range is
$14$ m. The paths induce connected and two-component periods; they do not
respond to tracking outcomes. All targets use a constant-velocity state
with independent per-step acceleration standard deviation $0.016$ m/s$^2$.
The filter uses a continuous white-acceleration covariance with intensity
$0.0128$ and survival probability $0.995$. Within FoV, detection probability is
$0.9$ and Cartesian observation noise has standard deviation $0.4$ m.
Poisson clutter has mean one per robot per frame and is uniform over a
fixed $104\times80$ m rectangle. This deliberately simple sensor model
does not include occlusion or robot localization error. Local pruning discards
existence at or below $0.01$; belief propagation uses tolerance $10^{-6}$
and at most 1,000 iterations. These settings are identical across arms.

The \emph{split--rejoin} scene has two initial targets and two targets born
at frame 35 while the teams are separated. The \emph{churn--departure} scene
uses repeated contact changes and removes one late target after frame 90;
the filters receive no departure notification. In the \emph{no-new} control,
only the two initial targets exist, although identical later birth priors
are still offered. Six fixed candidate regions have prior existence $0.1$,
position standard deviation $3.2$ m, and velocity standard deviation
$0.2$ m/s. Regions 1--2 are activated at frame 1 and regions 3--6 at frame
35. Regions 5--6 remain empty in every scene. Actual initial offsets and
target count are not given to the filter. Birth schedules and broad regions
are known, so the task is not unrestricted open-world discovery.

Development uses seeds 2801--2803 and is explicitly separated from
validation. Validation uses all seeds 2901--2920 in every family. Each
robot receives an independent constant path offset uniform in
$[-0.6,0.6]^2$ m, drawn before observations; physical components and trees
are recomputed. Constant offsets preserve speed and acceleration and
produce modest layout/contact variation. This is not twenty independently
designed navigation environments. Truth, detections, clutter, and directed
link-loss draws are paired across all arms. Directed packet loss is $0.1$.

\subsection{Controls and ablations}
The no-communication \emph{Local} arm provides context. \emph{FoV} uses the
existing observable-absence censor. \emph{Lineage} adds untouched-prior
exclusion; this is the main control for ER. \emph{Age-all} applies direct-age
weights to both spatial and existence pooling without lineage exclusion.
\emph{L+Age-all} additionally uses lineage exclusion, separating placement
of the age factor from qualification. \emph{ER} uses the same qualification
with age in existence alone. \emph{Confirmed ER} adds the two-hit gate.

The \emph{MIL-Z} control uses constrained LMB MIL,
$r=\sum_jw_jr_j$ and $p=\sum_jw_jr_jp_j/r$, with zero existence for absent
labels~\cite{gao2020mil}. \emph{MIL-S} specializes label-subspace MIL
\cite{gao2020mil,gao2022differentfov} to common label keys: for each
source-membership pattern, renormalize weights on represented sources
before the same formula. Its union of Gaussian components is reduced to
the common cap. Untouched but represented priors participate. This support
specialization does not reproduce the published independent-label
assignment pipeline, and neither MIL arm is a new algorithm.

Every nonlocal arm uses the same attempted links, delivered links, weights
before label processing, packet size, and control charge. The local update
and extraction are unchanged. All pre/post estimates and input caches are
saved. The original code and every experiment protocol are hash recorded.

\subsection{Metrics and uncertainty}
We report position OSPA with order $2$ and cutoff $12$ m
\cite{schuhmacher2008ospa}, cardinality absolute error, and GOSPA with
$p=2,c=12$ m,$\alpha=2$~\cite{gospa2017}. Localization, missed-target,
and false-target GOSPA components are reported as \emph{squared costs}
in m$^2$. All extant targets are scored at every robot, including targets
that are currently inaccessible. Thus a large error can represent a
communication-limited discovery failure rather than poor local sensing.

For localization comparisons, assignments are computed separately for each
method and matched errors are pooled only on robot--time--truth triples
matched within the cutoff by both methods. The RMSE and its support must
be read together; unmatched targets remain penalized by OSPA/GOSPA.
Departure false cost uses frames 91--120. Reunion OSPA uses the next ten
frames, truncated at a further disconnection. For opposite-subteam robots,
acquisition is the first of three consecutive assignments within 2 m of a
late target, censored at that target's departure or the episode end. This
measures geometric discovery, not label continuity. A separate Python evaluator
exhaustively enumerates the small rectangular assignments and checks all
saved MATLAB set metrics, independently of its Hungarian implementation.
This is an independent metric implementation, not an independent research
replication.

Uncertainty uses the twenty paired episodes as units, not the individual
robots or frames. We report paired mean differences and descriptive 95\%
percentile bootstrap intervals from 10,000 seed resamples. Intervals are
not adjusted for multiple comparisons and are not presented as confirmatory
significance tests. No validation outcome changes a method, threshold,
birth prior, path, or included seed.

\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
The useful result is conditional: direct-opportunity recency can improve
existence decisions without also changing spatial weights. With a retained
lineage qualifier, this improves global set estimation in the studied
robot encounters and avoids the common-target localization degradation of
both-block age weighting. It does not solve every failure induced by
intermittent sensing. In particular, observation lineage can preserve an
incorrect hypothesis, and a robot with old direct age may carry valuable
relayed evidence. A confirmation gate can reject useful short-contact
discoveries as well as false hypotheses. The failed development gate and
reversed departure result make that limitation explicit.

The main threats to generality are the small target population, shared
birth schedule and label keys, known global poses, simplified Cartesian
detections, mean-based FoV tests, fixed paths, one fusion round, and limited
mixture representation. Unknown birth locations, independent-label
association, occlusion, localization error, sensor heterogeneity, and
real robot execution are not validated. The simulations also use a reliable
central geometry coordinator with modeled control traffic. Neither
network-protocol feasibility nor closed-loop navigation follows from them.

Within this scope, observation history should be treated as distinct from
spatial information when designing a fusion rule for intermittently
connected robot teams. The present evidence supports existence-only age
weighting as a small fusion-layer change, with an explicit false-track
tradeoff. Broader deployment requires evaluating that tradeoff under
realistic birth, sensing, and label-association processes.

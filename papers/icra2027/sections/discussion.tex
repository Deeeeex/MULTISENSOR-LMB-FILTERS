\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
ER uses observation history to select participating inputs and direct
sensing age to weight existence, while retaining ordinary spatial weights.
Across the three mobile-robot scenarios, this separation reduces set error
relative to ER without recency. In split--rejoin and churn--departure, it
also avoids the common-target localization degradation of applying age to
both blocks. A robot's direct sensing age
and the spatial information accumulated in its posterior describe different
properties: earlier observations and relayed evidence can remain useful
after direct contact ends.

False-track rejection introduces a further tradeoff. ER retains some false
hypotheses, and the two-hit confirmation gate reduces no-new false cost at
the expense of discovery during short sensing contacts. The departure and
acquisition results show why set error, localization, false cost, and
discovery delay should be evaluated together. An adaptive confirmation rule
that accounts for sensing-contact duration is a concrete direction for
extending this approach.

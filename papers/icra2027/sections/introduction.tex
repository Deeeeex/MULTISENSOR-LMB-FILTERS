\section{Introduction}
Mobile sensors encounter different targets, occlusions, and communication
gaps. When two robots exchange tracks, a recently received posterior can
contain old information, whereas a low existence probability can encode a
useful current missed detection. A fusion rule must decide which part of
each belief to trust without exchanging the entire observation history.
This matters in cooperative vehicle tracking, where uncertain target
number, independent track births, and brief communication opportunities
occur together.

Labeled multi-Bernoulli (LMB) filtering represents a track by its label,
existence probability, and conditional spatial density
\cite{reuter2014lmb}. Geometric pooling, or Kullback--Leibler averaging
(KLA), combines such posteriors when cross-correlations are unavailable
\cite{battistelli2014kla}. A normalized geometric pool retains one common
prior in the ideal shared-prior case, but also tempers each sensor's new
likelihood. Changing posterior weights according to observation age does
not separately control the inherited and current contributions. Moreover,
the existence probability depends on the integral of the spatial product:
correcting only existence log-odds generally omits part of the same
Bernoulli evidence.

We propose \emph{guarded current-evidence fusion} (GCE). Each robot
retains its prediction and local update long enough to encode their
Bernoulli ratio. At a communication event, GCE starts from a conservative
pool and supplements it with admitted current ratios. The ratios modify
both the spatial distribution and its contribution to existence
normalization. Admission depends on current sensing opportunity,
association-weighted detection-score support, and whether the
moment-matched Gaussian ratio has admissible curvature.

Separating prior information from likelihood information is established
\cite{wu2022sharedpriors,hlinka2012likelihood}. Our contribution is a
guarded Bernoulli realization for approximate local tracking with unequal
sensing histories, together with experiments that isolate the spatial
correction from the scalar correction and the local detection-score model.
We do not assume that the two vehicles' detector errors or inherited
posteriors are independent.

The paper makes three contributions:
\begin{itemize}
 \item A normalized Bernoulli correction that applies the same admitted
 current evidence to existence and spatial density, with a tractable
 Gaussian form and an explicit integrability fallback.
 \item A bounded admission rule for approximate local updates and a
 bit-preserving codec that omits exactly zero Gaussian ratios.
 \item A complete two-condition, 25-sequence comparison with shared
 score-aware local filtering, external fusion adaptations, component
 ablations, common-target localization, and actual encoded byte counts.
\end{itemize}
The main result is an improvement of the complete fusion rule on this
development corpus. In particular, the small contribution of conservative
history weighting remains uncertain; it is not used to explain all of the
observed gain.

\section{Related Work}
\subsection{Cooperative robotic tracking}
Distributed PHD filtering supports robot search and tracking
\cite{dames2020search}, while multirobot reconfiguration addresses changes
in sensing resources~\cite{ramachandran2021reconfiguration}.
LMB representations also support distributed robot landmark mapping
\cite{gao2026lmbslam}. These works establish the broader robotic setting;
our experiment keeps sensor trajectories and local dynamics fixed and
changes posterior fusion. For cooperative driving, V2V4Real provides
real multi-vehicle observations~\cite{xu2023v2v4real}, and DMSTrack learns
uncertainty within a differentiable multisensor tracker
\cite{chiu2024dmstrack}. We use its released per-vehicle detections to
study a planar LMB fusion problem, without reproducing that learned
tracker or claiming an official 3-D benchmark result.

\subsection{Finite-set fusion with different sensing support}
Different fields of view require care with absent labels and exclusive
information~\cite{li2018multiviewlmb,gao2022differentfov}.
Minimum-information-loss (MIL) fusion uses arithmetic pooling of labeled
finite-set densities~\cite{gao2020mil}. Track consensus instead matches
and combines short trajectories, including tracks seen by only some
sensors~\cite{nguyen2021tc}. Our comparisons include an implementation
of the public MIL author manuscript~\cite{gao2019milpreprint} and the
author's track-consensus functions under the same local detections.
Their original experiments and architectures differ from this replay.

Separating cardinality and localization in finite-set fusion is also
established: pointwise density consistency alone does not ensure a good
cardinality distribution~\cite{uney2019cardinality}.
Heterogeneous fusion can assign confidence at the component level
\cite{yi2021heterogeneous}.
GCE addresses a narrower question: after choosing an inherited base,
how should an admitted local Bernoulli update change both the spatial
density and the probability that the target exists?

\subsection{Prior and current likelihood information}
Shared-prior Bayesian fusion studies the correction needed when local
posteriors contain common prior information~\cite{wu2022sharedpriors}.
Likelihood consensus aggregates exponential-family likelihood
representations across sensors~\cite{hlinka2012likelihood}.
These principles motivate transmitting current Gaussian natural-parameter
increments. GCE uses fractional, source-specific increments because local
LMB association and moment projection need not yield an exact independent
likelihood. Its curvature guard is an implementation safeguard for that
approximation, not a proof that the observation sources are independent.

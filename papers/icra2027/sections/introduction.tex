\section{Introduction}
Mobile robots often acquire complementary views while moving between
connected and disconnected regions. When two subteams meet again, a newly
received track can be supported by recent local measurements, inherited
from an earlier encounter, or still close to an unobserved birth prior.
Conversely, a low existence probability can reflect a useful recent missed
detection or merely a lack of sensing opportunity. These distinctions matter
when the communication opportunity permits only a small number of fusion
rounds. The information exchanged over a newly available link can therefore
differ substantially in recency. Figure~\ref{fig:overview} summarizes the
proposed fusion mechanism.

Random finite set filters provide a natural representation of uncertain
target number and state. The labeled multi-Bernoulli (LMB) filter represents
each candidate track by an existence probability and a conditional spatial
density~\cite{reuter2014lmb}. Geometric pooling, also called generalized
covariance intersection or Kullback--Leibler averaging (KLA), is widely used
when cross-correlations are unavailable~\cite{battistelli2014kla}.
Unequal fields of view (FoVs) create differences in label support and
observation history. We ask: \emph{where should direct-observation recency enter
a Bernoulli fusion rule when robots reunite?}

Multiplying a source's entire fusion weight by an age factor seems natural,
but changes both existence and position pooling. A source that has not
observed a track recently can still carry a useful spatial estimate received
from another robot. Reducing its spatial influence solely because its own
sensor has been uninformative can therefore sacrifice localization while
improving target-number estimates. We study an existence-only alternative:
retain ordinary spatial weights on eligible inputs, and use direct-opportunity
age to weight the Bernoulli existence terms while retaining the spatial
overlap normalizer.

We make three contributions. First, we introduce existence recency (ER):
an observation-history rule qualifies fusion inputs, and the time since a
robot's last direct sensing opportunity determines its existence weight.
Second, we derive the resulting Bernoulli pool from a two-block variational
formulation and establish its fixed-input spatial invariance. Third, we
evaluate transfer to public two-vehicle real detections and use two
eight-robot event families as mechanism case studies, with external fusion
comparisons and ablations of qualification, recency placement, and confirmation.

\section{Related Work}
\subsection{Robot sensor fusion}
Robot sensor fusion uses representations suited to the estimated state.
The ROS robot\_localization package provides generalized extended Kalman
filter (EKF) fusion~\cite{moore2016robotlocalization}, while LIO-SAM
organizes lidar and inertial constraints in a factor graph
\cite{shan2020liosam}. For dynamic-object tracking, Autoware combines
data association with EKF state updates~\cite{autoware2026tracker}.
LMB representations also support robotic mapping: Gao \emph{et al.}
develop distributed multi-robot SLAM with LMB landmark maps and map fusion,
evaluated on simulated and real data~\cite{gao2026lmbslam}.
In our target-tracking setting, labeled Bernoullis represent object
identity, existence, and state, while ER determines how robots combine
their posteriors.

\subsection{Cooperative robotic search and tracking}
Dames studies distributed search and tracking with PHD filtering and robot
motion~\cite{dames2020search}. Ramachandran \emph{et al.} reconfigure a
multirobot communication network after sensing-resource degradation,
using a central optimization system~\cite{ramachandran2021reconfiguration}.
DecSTER combines particle PHD filtering and Thompson-sampling action
selection for decentralized search and tracking with unreliable,
asynchronous inter-agent communication~\cite{decster2024}. These works
address decisions about where robots should move or how resources should
be connected. We hold motion and attempted links fixed to isolate the
effect of posterior fusion during intermittent contact.

\subsection{Unequal views and information weighting}
Wang \emph{et al.} use information divergence to adapt LMB fusion
weights across multiple views~\cite{wang2018multiview}. FoV compensation
and multi-view geometric fusion are also established approaches
\cite{li2018multiviewlmb,li2020multiview}. Gao \emph{et al.} derive
LMB-constrained minimum-information-loss (MIL) pooling and decompose label
spaces for unequal views~\cite{gao2020mil,gao2022differentfov}.
Li \emph{et al.} explicitly recognize that retained or relayed information
can remain useful outside the current nominal FoV, and develop a
density-decomposition CPHD approach~\cite{li2021cphd}.
Jin \emph{et al.} examine shared-prior label consistency and introduce a
consensus information selector~\cite{jin2024selector}. Communication-aware
adaptive KLA/LMB weights account for network constraints
\cite{lang2026adaptive}, while label assignment methods address
inconsistent identities across nodes~\cite{li2019labelmatching}.
Nguyen \emph{et al.} use finite-history trajectory consensus under limited
FoV, maintaining local filtering independently of fused tracks
\cite{nguyen2021tc}. We include this distinct fusion architecture explicitly.
Building on these approaches, we examine where a direct-observation age
factor should enter the Bernoulli fusion recursion.

\subsection{Existence and spatial fusion}
\"Uney \emph{et al.} show that pointwise consistency of finite-set geometric
mixtures does not ensure cardinality consistency, and advocate separate
cardinality and localization fusion~\cite{uney2019cardinality}. Our rule
retains the spatial overlap penalty while assigning distinct weights to
spatial densities and Bernoulli existence terms. This formulation lets us
isolate the effect of observation recency on existence decisions and
examine the resulting discovery--localization tradeoff at robot encounters.

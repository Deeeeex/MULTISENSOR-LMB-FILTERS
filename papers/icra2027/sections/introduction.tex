\section{Introduction}
Cooperative robots observe different targets, occlusions, and missed
detections. Their exchanged tracks also contain information inherited
from earlier communication. A recently received posterior may therefore
carry old evidence, while a low existence probability may convey a useful
current miss. Effective fusion must distinguish inherited beliefs from
new observations without transmitting the entire sensing history.

Labeled multi-Bernoulli (LMB) filtering represents each track by a label,
existence probability, and conditional spatial density
\cite{vo2013conjugate,reuter2014lmb}. Geometric pooling, or Kullback--Leibler averaging
(KLA), combines local posteriors when their cross-correlations are
unavailable~\cite{battistelli2014kla}. In the ideal shared-prior case,
normalized geometric weights retain one copy of the prior but also temper
each new likelihood. Changing posterior weights according to observation
age does not separately control these contributions. A further coupling
arises within each Bernoulli: the integral of its spatial product affects
the probability that the target exists. An existence correction alone
leaves this part of the current evidence unused.
Figure~\ref{fig:intro} illustrates the distinction: a posterior weight
acts on inherited and current information together, whereas a current
ratio can change their relative contributions.

We propose \emph{guarded current-evidence fusion} (GCE). Each robot encodes
the ratio between its local update and prediction. Fusion augments a
conservative posterior pool with admitted fractions of these ratios,
then jointly normalizes existence and spatial density. Current sensing
opportunity, association-weighted score support, and Gaussian curvature
determine which increments are admitted. Figure~\ref{fig:overview} shows
how the same current evidence reaches both branches of the fused belief.


Prior--likelihood separation is established
\cite{wu2022sharedpriors,hlinka2012likelihood}. GCE develops this principle
into a bounded Bernoulli correction for approximate local tracking with
unequal sensing histories. The paper contributes:
\begin{itemize}
 \item A joint existence and spatial correction with closed-form Gaussian
 normalization and a source-level curvature guard.
 \item An admission rule that combines current association, detection-score,
 and missed-detection evidence, plus an exact codec for zero increments.
 \item A cooperative tracking evaluation with common local filtering,
 geometric and arithmetic fusion references, component ablations, and
 measured packet sizes.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\subsection{Cooperative robotic tracking}
Distributed probability hypothesis density (PHD) filters support robot
search~\cite{dames2020search}, decentralized action selection
\cite{decster2024}, and sensing reconfiguration
\cite{ramachandran2021reconfiguration}; LMB filters also support
distributed mapping~\cite{gao2026lmbslam}. V2V4Real provides real
cooperative driving observations~\cite{xu2023v2v4real}, and DMSTrack
learns uncertainty in a differentiable multisensor tracker
\cite{chiu2024dmstrack}.

Where2comm selects spatial features using confidence maps
\cite{hu2022where2comm}; CoopTrack exchanges instance features and learns
cross-agent association~\cite{zhong2025cooptrack}. GCE instead operates
on probabilistic track summaries. We evaluate this fusion layer using
DMSTrack's released per-vehicle detections.

\subsection{Finite-set fusion and sensing support}
Covariance intersection addresses fusion with unknown cross-correlations
\cite{julier1997ci}, and KLA formulates geometric pooling over probability
densities~\cite{battistelli2014kla}. Generalized covariance intersection
(GCI) extends this approach to PHD filters through exponential mixtures
\cite{uney2013emd}, while consensus labeled random finite set (RFS)
filters exchange multi-object beliefs with track identities
\cite{fantacci2015consensus}.

Direct GCI fusion can degrade under inconsistent labels
\cite{li2018robust}; linear assignment can align local identities before
labelwise fusion~\cite{li2019labelmatching}.
Different fields of view require explicit treatment of absent labels and
exclusive information
\cite{li2018multiviewlmb,li2020multiview,gao2022differentfov}.
Minimum-information-loss (MIL) fusion uses arithmetic pooling of labeled
finite-set densities~\cite{gao2020mil}; track consensus matches and
combines short trajectories~\cite{nguyen2021tc}. These methods motivate
our use of aligned labels and explicit sensing support.

Cardinality and localization also play distinct roles in finite-set
fusion: pointwise density consistency alone does not ensure a useful
cardinality distribution~\cite{uney2019cardinality}. Heterogeneous fusion
assigns confidence at the component level~\cite{yi2021heterogeneous}.
GCE instead couples the two components through the normalizer of an
admitted Bernoulli update.

\subsection{Prior and current likelihood information}
Shared-prior Bayesian fusion corrects for common information in local
posteriors~\cite{wu2022sharedpriors}. Likelihood consensus aggregates
exponential-family likelihood representations~\cite{hlinka2012likelihood}.
GCE transmits Gaussian natural-parameter increments and admits them
fractionally. This accommodates local LMB association and moment
projection, for which the posterior-to-prediction ratio can differ from
an exact sensor likelihood.

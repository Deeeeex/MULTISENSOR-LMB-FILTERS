\subsection{Transfer to real detections}
Table~\ref{tab:real} reports all nine sequences. With reliable links,
ER's macro-average OSPA is \RealErReliable\ m, compared with
\RealMilReliable\ m for MIL-AM and \RealTcFiveReliable/\RealTcTenReliable\ m
for TC windows five/ten. With intermittent links, the corresponding values
are \RealErIntermittent, \RealMilIntermittent, and
\RealTcFiveIntermittent/\RealTcTenIntermittent\ m.
ER beats MIL-AM in seven of nine reliable-link sequences and eight of nine
intermittent-link sequences, and each TC window in eight of nine sequences
under either condition. Sequence 0004 favors MIL-AM and TC over ER.

The age factor itself does not yield a consistent transfer gain.
Relative to ER w/o age, paired OSPA differences are
$+\RealDeltaReliable$ m [\RealDeltaLowReliable, \RealDeltaHighReliable]
with reliable links and $+\RealDeltaIntermittent$ m
[\RealDeltaLowIntermittent, \RealDeltaHighIntermittent] with intermittent
links. Both intervals include zero. ER improves OSPA in five of nine
sequences under each condition, but increases mean false-target cost by
6.73 and 4.46 m$^2$, respectively. Thus the external-method comparison
supports the full fusion configuration in this replay; it does not isolate
a benefit of recency. Figure~\ref{fig:real} retains every sequence comparison.

\subsection{Case studies: existence recency and set estimation}
Tables~\ref{tab:main} and~\ref{tab:ablation} separate external/control
comparisons from mechanism ablations.
Relative to ER without recency, ER reduces mean OSPA from \LineageSplit\ to
\ErSplit\ m in split--rejoin and from \LineageChurn\ to \ErChurn\ m in
churn--departure. The paired differences are
\DeltaSplit\ m [\DeltaLowSplit, \DeltaHighSplit] and
\DeltaChurn\ m [\DeltaLowChurn, \DeltaHighChurn], respectively.
In the no-new control, the difference is
\DeltaNonew\ m [\DeltaLowNonew, \DeltaHighNonew].
Brackets denote the 95\% bootstrap intervals defined above.
Cardinality error decreases in all three scenarios. In the
reunion windows, OSPA is 2.533 versus 3.264 m in split--rejoin and 0.926
versus 1.248 m in churn--departure (ER versus ER w/o age).

FoV, MIL-Z/S, and both TC windows have higher OSPA than ER in these
case studies. TC uses independent local histories and no density feedback,
whereas recursive density fusion relays information over successive tree
edges. Since every robot is scored against the global target set, this
architectural difference contributes to the large TC gap; it cannot be
attributed to the age factor. Support partitioning improves MIL in the two
event cases. Local has the largest OSPA, consistent with its limited coverage.

\subsection{Where the age factor belongs}
Table~\ref{tab:ablation} and Fig.~\ref{fig:outcomes}(b) distinguish existence
gains from localization. With the same observation-history qualification,
ER (both) increases common-target RMSE relative to ER w/o age by 16.8\%
in split--rejoin and 3.1\% in churn--departure. ER leaves this RMSE almost
unchanged, with ratios of 0.9999 and 0.9998. This agrees with the motivation in
Fig.~\ref{fig:mechanism}: recent direct sensor opportunity is useful for
existence weighting, while a posterior also retains spatial information
from earlier observations and robot encounters.

\subsection{False hypotheses and the confirmation tradeoff}
Table~\ref{tab:ablation} shows the false-hypothesis tradeoff. ER's mean
post-departure false cost is 12.255 m$^2$, compared with 10.785 m$^2$ for
ER w/o age. The paired difference is $+1.470$ m$^2$
[$-0.705$, $3.795$].
Similarly, ER's no-new false cost is 0.570 m$^2$, versus 0.00375 m$^2$
for FoV. Lower OSPA therefore does not imply better false-track rejection.

Confirmed ER reduces no-new false cost to 0.00375 m$^2$, but its churn
OSPA is 1.519 m, versus ER's 0.846 m. This is consistent with the cost of
requiring consecutive local positive associations during short sensing
opportunities. In churn, ER acquires 160/160 opposite-subteam robot--target
queries, while Confirmed ER acquires 152/160. Among successful acquisitions,
mean delays are 2.47 and 7.63 s, respectively. The remaining eight Confirmed
ER queries are unacquired by their censoring time.
Discovery and post-departure persistence therefore require separate metrics.

\subsection{Communication cost}
In the case studies, all communicating methods use the same attempted and delivered
messages for each paired trial. Mean attempted wire cost is 24.98 MiB
for split--rejoin and no-new, and 25.74 MiB for churn--departure. Across communicating methods and
scenarios, mean unpadded payload totals range from 2.14 to 9.85 MiB;
the padded message budget is identical. TC's raw payloads are 0.68--1.76 MiB.
In the real replay, equal packet opportunities yield different fragment
costs: mean attempted wire volume per sequence is 8.22/8.24 MiB for ER,
9.97/9.85 MiB for MIL-AM, and 6.97/6.97 MiB for either TC window
(reliable/intermittent). ER's raw payloads are 4.35/4.48 MiB, compared with
1.35/2.64 MiB for TC windows five/ten. This is not an equal-byte comparison.

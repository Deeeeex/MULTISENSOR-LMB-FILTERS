\subsection{Existence recency and set estimation}
Table~\ref{tab:main} compares set estimation across the three scenarios.
Relative to ER without recency, ER reduces mean OSPA from \LineageSplit\ to
\ErSplit\ m in split--rejoin and from \LineageChurn\ to \ErChurn\ m in
churn--departure. The paired differences are
\DeltaSplit\ m [\DeltaLowSplit, \DeltaHighSplit] and
\DeltaChurn\ m [\DeltaLowChurn, \DeltaHighChurn], respectively.
In the no-new control, the difference is
\DeltaNonew\ m [\DeltaLowNonew, \DeltaHighNonew].
Brackets denote the 95\% bootstrap intervals defined above.
Cardinality error decreases in all three scenarios. In the
reunion windows, OSPA is 2.533 versus 3.264 m in split--rejoin and 0.926
versus 1.248 m in churn--departure (ER versus ER w/o age).

FoV and the two MIL controls have higher OSPA than ER. Support partitioning
improves MIL in the two event scenarios, while both controls incur larger
localization and false-target costs. Local has the largest OSPA, consistent
with evaluating global target sets while robots are separated.

\subsection{Where the age factor belongs}
Table~\ref{tab:common} and Fig.~\ref{fig:outcomes}(b) distinguish existence
gains from localization. With the same observation-history qualification,
ER (both) increases common-target RMSE relative to ER w/o age by 16.8\%
in split--rejoin and 3.1\% in churn--departure. ER leaves this RMSE almost
unchanged, with ratios of 0.9999 and 0.9998. This agrees with the motivation in
Fig.~\ref{fig:mechanism}: recent direct sensor opportunity is useful for
existence weighting, while a posterior also retains spatial information
from earlier observations and robot encounters.

\subsection{False hypotheses and the confirmation tradeoff}
Table~\ref{tab:false} shows the false-hypothesis tradeoff. ER's mean
post-departure false cost is 12.255 m$^2$, compared with 10.785 m$^2$ for
ER w/o age. The paired difference is $+1.470$ m$^2$
[$-0.705$, $3.795$].
Similarly, ER's no-new false cost is 0.570 m$^2$, versus 0.00375 m$^2$
for FoV. Lower OSPA therefore does not imply better false-track rejection.

Confirmed ER reduces no-new false cost to 0.00375 m$^2$, but its churn
OSPA is 1.519 m, versus ER's 0.846 m. This is consistent with the cost of
requiring consecutive local positive associations during short sensing
opportunities. In churn, ER acquires 160/160 opposite-subteam robot--target
queries, while Confirmed ER acquires 152/160. Among successful acquisitions,
mean delays are 2.47 and 7.63 s, respectively. The remaining eight Confirmed
ER queries are unacquired by their censoring time.
The unsmoothed trajectories in Fig.~\ref{fig:time} show why discovery and
post-departure persistence should be evaluated separately.

\subsection{Communication cost}
All communicating methods use the same attempted and delivered
messages for each paired trial. Mean attempted wire cost is 24.98 MiB
for split--rejoin and no-new, and 25.74 MiB for churn--departure. Across communicating methods and
scenarios, mean unpadded payload totals range from 2.14 to 9.85 MiB;
the padded message budget is identical.

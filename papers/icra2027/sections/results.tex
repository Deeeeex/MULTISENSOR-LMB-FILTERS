\subsection{Fusion on the previously used corpus}
GCE has the lowest mean OSPA among the main comparisons
(Table~\ref{tab:main}), improving on No-age KLA by
\GainNoAgeReliable\%/\GainNoAgeIntermittent\% and on Recency by
\GainRecencyReliable\%/\GainRecencyIntermittent\%
(reliable/intermittent). Against No-age, missed-target cost falls by
\MissGainNoAgeReliable\%/\MissGainNoAgeIntermittent\% and false-target
cost by \FalseGainNoAgeReliable\%/\FalseGainNoAgeIntermittent\%.
Common-target RMSE decreases from \LocNoAgeReliable\ to
\LocMainNoAgeReliable\ m and from \LocNoAgeIntermittent\ to
\LocMainNoAgeIntermittent\ m. These are fusion comparisons on the
development corpus, not an independent estimate of end-to-end
generalization.

GCE--Fixed Ratio is
\RevSeenDeltaFixedReliableMean/\RevSeenDeltaFixedIntermittentMean\ m.
Both paired intervals lie
below zero. This supports adaptive score/miss admission relative to
the selected constant-strength control; it does not rule out a better
fixed strength outside the three-point development grid.

\subsection{What the recursive controls identify}
Guarded Scalar narrows the gap previously attributed to joint correction
(Table~\ref{tab:ablation}). GCE--GS is
\RevSeenDeltaGSReliableMean/\RevSeenDeltaGSIntermittentMean\ m,
with intervals
$[\RevSeenDeltaGSReliableLow,\RevSeenDeltaGSReliableHigh]$ and
$[\RevSeenDeltaGSIntermittentLow,\RevSeenDeltaGSIntermittentHigh]$.
The intermittent upper bound is within $10^{-4}$ m of zero.
On the nine development sequences, the intermittent GCE--GS interval
includes zero.

The correction--curvature interaction is
\RevSeenInteractionReliableMean/\RevSeenInteractionIntermittentMean\ m,
with intervals
$[\RevSeenInteractionReliableLow,\RevSeenInteractionReliableHigh]$ and
$[\RevSeenInteractionIntermittentLow,\RevSeenInteractionIntermittentHigh]$.
Both include zero, providing no distinct interaction benefit or
normalizer-only explanation of the gain.
Removing curvature allows large position shifts and activates aggregate
fallback twice. Removing positive score support trades fewer misses
for more false targets; the history and score-removal total-OSPA
intervals include zero.

On identical GCE inputs, changing only the spatial mean at corrected
existence reduces immediate OSPA by about 0.003 m. The joint
base/base-to-corrected/corrected difference is
\JointReliableBothMean/\JointIntermittentBothMean\ m, and its intervals
include zero, as do those for changing the integral at corrected means.
These substitutions hold admission fixed and never change future
predictions. They answer a different question from the fully recursive
GS comparison.

\subsection{New segments and the generalization limit}
Across all three newly acquired segments, GCE has the lowest mean OSPA
among the seven evaluated methods (Table~\ref{tab:newdata}).
However, two segments come from an already represented recording. On the only previously absent recording, GCE worsens OSPA to
\RevNewRecordingGCEReliable/\RevNewRecordingGCEIntermittent\ m,
compared with No-age's
\RevNewRecordingNoAgeReliable/\RevNewRecordingNoAgeIntermittent\ m
and Scalar's
\RevNewRecordingScalarReliable/\RevNewRecordingScalarIntermittent\ m.
The aggregate gain therefore does not establish stable generalization
to new recordings. All settings and segments are retained.

\subsection{Motion and detection-probability sensitivity}
With planar ego-motion compensation, GCE--GS on the 25 sequences is
\RevMotionSeenDeltaGSReliableMean/\RevMotionSeenDeltaGSIntermittentMean\ m,
with paired intervals
$[\RevMotionSeenDeltaGSReliableLow,\RevMotionSeenDeltaGSReliableHigh]$/
$[\RevMotionSeenDeltaGSIntermittentLow,\RevMotionSeenDeltaGSIntermittentHigh]$
below zero. Absolute error does not improve uniformly. On the new
recording, GCE reaches
\RevMotionNewRecordingGCEReliable/\RevMotionNewRecordingGCEIntermittent\ m,
compared with No-age's
\RevMotionNewRecordingNoAgeReliable/\RevMotionNewRecordingNoAgeIntermittent\ m
and Scalar's
\RevMotionNewRecordingScalarReliable/\RevMotionNewRecordingScalarIntermittent\ m.
It now beats No-age but remains worse than Scalar. These runs change
the coordinate model for every method without retuning admission.
Across all four modeled $p_D$ values, GCE retains a lower mean than
No-age and GS, but Scalar wins at reliable $p_D=0.8$ and intermittent
$p_D\in\{0.7,0.95\}$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:robustness}a,b).
Varying a constant $p_D$ cannot validate visibility under occlusion.
All sequence-level sensitivity results, including the nine development
sequences and each new segment, remain available in the companion evidence.

\subsection{Integrability does not ensure calibrated uncertainty}
Every precision increment in the Gaussian correlation control is
positive semidefinite. At zero correlation, unit-admission GCE and the
known-correlation oracle agree exactly. At $\varrho=0.9$, GCE's nominal
95\% regions cover only \RevCorrelationGCECoverage\% of samples, versus
\RevCorrelationOracleCoverage\% for the oracle
(Fig.~\ref{fig:robustness}c). Its mean normalized squared error per
dimension is \RevCorrelationGCENEES. The guard therefore certifies an
admissible Gaussian correction, not reliability under unknown
cross-source correlation. Covariance coverage with adaptive gates remains
unmeasured.

\subsection{Communication cost}
The codec preserves full GCE trajectories and reduces raw payload by
\CodecRawSavingReliable\%/\CodecRawSavingIntermittent\%.
Fragmentation and control traffic reduce this saving to
\CodecWireSavingReliable\%/\CodecWireSavingIntermittent\%
(Table~\ref{tab:communication}). Relative to native No-age KLA, encoded
GCE still requires
\CodecRawExtraNoAgeReliable\%/\CodecRawExtraNoAgeIntermittent\% more raw
payload and
\CodecWireExtraNoAgeReliable\%/\CodecWireExtraNoAgeIntermittent\% more
fragmented bytes. GS and Fixed Ratio use the same full 352-byte
per-Bernoulli schema as GCE; native Scalar uses 232 bytes. Packet-length-independent loss emulation does not
measure wireless latency, channel capacity, or energy.

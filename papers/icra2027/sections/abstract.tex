We propose guarded current-evidence fusion (GCE) for cooperative
multitarget tracking with intermittent communication. A robot's posterior
mixes inherited information with its latest local measurement update;
observation age alone does not distinguish these contributions. GCE retains
a conservative posterior pool and admits a bounded fraction of each
current Bernoulli posterior-to-prediction ratio. The same admitted ratio
updates the spatial density and the existence normalizer, yielding a
closed-form Gaussian implementation. A curvature test rejects unsuitable
moment-matched increments, while a zero-vector codec preserves every
transmitted floating-point value. We evaluate released two-vehicle
V2V4Real detections with a shared score-aware local tracker. On a complete
25-sequence evaluation, GCE obtains OSPA of \MainReliable\ and
\MainIntermittent\ m under reliable and intermittent links, respectively.
These are \GainNoAgeReliable\% and \GainNoAgeIntermittent\% below
qualified geometric pooling without age, with both paired 95\% sequence
intervals below zero. Joint spatial and existence correction also improves
over a scalar-only correction in both conditions. Missed-target and
false-target costs decrease relative to the no-age and recency controls,
and common-target localization improves. Exact zero omission reduces
fragmented communication relative to the full Gaussian packet, although
the resulting payload remains larger than scalar summaries. All real
sequences informed development, so these results establish a corpus-level
fusion improvement rather than independent generalization.

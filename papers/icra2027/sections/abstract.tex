Intermittent contact between mobile robots brings together target beliefs
with different observation histories. Weighting an entire Bernoulli density
by direct-observation age can improve existence decisions while degrading
spatial pooling. We propose existence recency (ER), a fusion rule for labeled
multi-Bernoulli tracking that screens untouched priors, retains ordinary
spatial weights, and applies observation recency to existence terms.
Both detections and missed detections update the local observation age,
which remains unchanged by relaying. A two-block variational formulation
establishes that this weighting preserves the fused spatial density for
fixed inputs. Across three eight-robot simulation scenarios with twenty
Monte Carlo trials each, ER reduces mean OSPA by \GainSplit\% and
\GainChurn\% relative to its no-recency ablation in split--rejoin and
churn--departure scenarios. Common-target localization RMSE remains within
0.02\% of that ablation, whereas applying recency to both blocks increases
it by 16.8\% and 3.1\%. ER yields higher post-departure false-target cost;
adding two-hit confirmation reduces false hypotheses at the
cost of slower discovery. These results identify a tradeoff between timely
existence decisions and spatial information retention in multirobot fusion.

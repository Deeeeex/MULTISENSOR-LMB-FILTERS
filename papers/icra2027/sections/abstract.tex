Intermittent contact between mobile robots brings together target beliefs
with different observation histories. Weighting an entire Bernoulli density
by direct-observation age can improve existence decisions while degrading
spatial pooling. We study a lightweight alternative in an existing labeled
multi-Bernoulli tracker: qualify observation lineage, retain ordinary spatial
weights, and apply direct-opportunity recency only to existence terms.
Recent missed detections remain valid evidence, and relays do not refresh
local direct age. A two-block variational formulation establishes the
fixed-input spatial invariance of this rule, without claiming recursive
accuracy or cardinality consistency. In three prescribed eight-robot
simulation families with twenty new paired seeds each, the rule reduces
mean OSPA by \GainSplit\% and \GainChurn\% against a lineage-aware control
in split--rejoin and churn--departure scenarios. Common-target localization
RMSE remains within 0.02\% of the control, whereas applying age to both
blocks increases it by 16.8\% and 3.1\%. False-target behavior remains a
limitation: the departure benefit seen during development does not reproduce,
and confirmation trades fewer false hypotheses for slower discovery.
The findings concern the placement of temporal trust in posterior fusion
under shared birth labels and known poses, not a complete robotic perception
or navigation system.

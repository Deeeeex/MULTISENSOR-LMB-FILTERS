\begin{abstract}
We study sparse communication for distributed labeled multi-Bernoulli tracking
when mobile formations change their physical neighbors. A causal routing
policy retains a feasible formation tree, repairs it only when necessary, and
combines local directed cycles with bidirectional gateways. The scheduled
backbone uses $N+2(F-1)$ posterior messages for $N$ sensors in $F$ formations.
We separate planned connectivity from delivered, label-specific fusion.
Paired 160-step development experiments with 24 and 36 sensors reduce
attempted posterior bytes by 10.0\% and 21.8\% relative to fixed routing.
Mean OSPA decreases by 2.40\% and 0.37\%, respectively; larger conditional
localization gains do not imply comparable target-set recovery. A post-hoc
error budget shows that fixed-cardinality localization improvements alone
can reduce mean OSPA by at most 0.28 and 1.31\,m in the sparse arms.
A receiver ablation exposes a precision--consistency tradeoff.
\end{abstract}
\begin{keywords}
Multi-object tracking, labeled multi-Bernoulli, distributed fusion, dynamic
topology, packet loss
\end{keywords}

\section{Introduction}
Mobile formations exchange posteriors to track beyond individual fields of
view. Motion breaks routes; redundant inputs consume bandwidth. Which
messages support both object existence and location within limited rounds?

The labeled multi-Bernoulli (LMB) filter represents object existence and
spatial states \cite{reuter2014lmb}. Consensus LMB filtering uses
Kullback--Leibler averaging (KLA), or normalized geometric fusion
\cite{battistelli2014kla,fantacci2015consensus}. Unlike repeated consensus
on fixed inputs, tracking interleaves observations with few communication
rounds; scheduled connectivity guarantees neither delivery nor lower error.

Randomized gossip relates averaging time to matrix spectra
\cite{boyd2006gossip}; event-triggered Bayesian filtering uses posterior
divergence from a predicted last transmission \cite{battistelli2019event}.
Ramachandran et al. jointly reconfigure topology and fusion weights after
sensor-quality degradation, then reposition robots for coverage
\cite{ramachandran2021reconfiguration}. We do not claim route repair as new.
With prescribed motion, we study sparse messages, directed losses and
finite-round LMB fusion under unequal views.

Multi-view methods include GM-PHD compensation \cite{li2020multiview},
LMB fusion \cite{li2018multiviewlmb}, and per-label weighting
\cite{wang2018multiview}. Label matching \cite{li2019labelmatching}, LMB
minimum information loss \cite{gao2020mil}, separate cardinality/localization
objectives \cite{uney2019cardinality}, and exclusive-track handling
\cite{gao2022differentfov} are established estimator ideas. All our routing
arms share an implementation-specific absent-label FoV censor, not these
complete algorithms. We claim no new fusion objective.

We distinguish sparse scheduling, delivered weights and target-set recovery
through exact message counts, conditional sensitivity bounds and paired
full/sparse comparisons.
One opened seed per scale reveals development tradeoffs, not generalization.

\section{Sparse Causal LMB Routing}
\subsection{Fusion and communication model}
Sensor $i$ maintains an LMB posterior
$\pi_i=\{(r_i^\ell,p_i^\ell)\}_{\ell\in\mathbb L_i}$, where $r_i^\ell$ is
the existence probability and $p_i^\ell(x)$ is a normalized spatial density.
For a common active label and weights $w_j\geq0$, $\sum_jw_j=1$, exact KLA
satisfies \cite{fantacci2015consensus}
\begin{align}
\eta^\ell &= \int \prod_j p_j^\ell(x)^{w_j}\,dx,
&\bar p^\ell(x)&=\frac{\prod_jp_j^\ell(x)^{w_j}}{\eta^\ell},\label{eq:density}\\
\operatorname{logit}\bar r^\ell
&=\sum_j w_j\operatorname{logit}r_j^\ell+\log\eta^\ell.\label{eq:existence}
\end{align}
H\"older's inequality gives $\eta^\ell\leq1$: spatial disagreement can lower
existence log odds relative to their weighted input value. Routing changes
both information access and density compatibility.

Let $G_t^{\rm phys}$ describe current physical links, and let $A_t$ be the
scheduled directed graph, with rows denoting receivers and columns senders.
The controller observes current positions, link reliabilities and its
previous route, but no target truth or future trajectory. The controller
uses network-wide current geometry; its decentralized acquisition and
dissemination are outside the measured posterior-payload budget. Fusion is local.

\subsection{Causal tree repair and sparse inputs}
For $F$ formations, a formation edge is usable when current sensor pairs
realize its gateways within receiver input constraints. The policy retains
the preceding formation tree when feasible. Otherwise it selects a feasible
tree lexicographically: minimize replaced formation edges, maximize bottleneck
reliability, maximize total log reliability, then minimize distance. Local
cycles and gateway endpoints use current geometry; only the formation tree
is retained (Fig.~\ref{fig:method}).

The sparse policy retains one directed cycle inside each formation. Each
undirected edge of the formation tree is realized by one directed gateway
in each direction; the two directions need not use the same sensor pair.
Each receiver has one dominant local input (weight 0.70); gateway receivers
also have a cross-formation input (0.05). Remaining weight is assigned to
self. The full-repair reference has two nonself inputs per receiver;
removing its unused local residual returns 0.05 to self.

\noindent\textbf{Proposition 1 (scheduled backbone).}
If every local cycle and every directed gateway is physically feasible,
the scheduled sensor graph is strongly connected and contains exactly
\begin{equation}M=N+2(F-1)\label{eq:messages}\end{equation}
directed messages per round.
\emph{Proof.} The cycles contribute $N$ edges and directed paths within
each formation. The tree contributes $2(F-1)$ disjoint gateway edges;
concatenating local paths and gateways connects every ordered sensor pair.
This architecture-specific count is neither a global lower bound over
strongly connected digraphs nor a guarantee about delivered links.

\section{Packet Delivery and Finite-Round Fusion}
\subsection{Where a missing weight goes}
Let $W$ be a planned row-stochastic matrix with positive diagonal.
For receiver $i$, let $D_{ij}\in\{0,1\}$ indicate delivery, set $D_{ii}=1$,
and define missing weight mass $m_i=\sum_{j:D_{ij}=0}W_{ij}$.
The current reference implementation omits an unavailable input and uses
\begin{equation}
 R_{ij}=\frac{W_{ij}D_{ij}}{1-m_i}.\label{eq:renorm}
\end{equation}
This differs from the planned removal of a local edge. An existing
alternative receiver rule returns missing mass to self:
\begin{equation}
 S_{ij}=W_{ij}D_{ij}+m_i\mathbf1\{i=j\}.\label{eq:self}
\end{equation}
Both are row stochastic with identical row perturbation norm
$\|R_{i:}-W_{i:}\|_1=\|S_{i:}-W_{i:}\|_1=2m_i$.
Figure~\ref{fig:mechanism} shows how renormalization amplifies a surviving
input, changing overlap and existence. Self fallback avoids amplification
but can slow useful information transfer; neither rule is uniformly preferable.

Neither rule preserves double stochasticity under arbitrary directed loss.
A symmetric two-node matrix with off-diagonal weight $a$ becomes
$\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\a&1-a\end{smallmatrix}\right]$ under one-way
loss and self fallback; column sums are $1+a$ and $1-a$. Reciprocal
scheduled gateways therefore do not establish equal-weight KLA consensus.

\subsection{A finite-round sensitivity bound}
Freeze positive densities $q_j$ on a common support between fusion rounds,
and write $q_\alpha\propto\prod_jq_j^{\alpha_j}$ for a probability vector
$\alpha$. Assume $\osc(\log q_j)=\sup\log q_j-\inf\log q_j\leq L$.
Let $P=\widetilde W_K\cdots\widetilde W_1$ and $Q=W_K\cdots W_1$ be
delivered and planned products. For either receiver rule,
\begin{align}
 D_{\rm KL}(q_{P_{i:}}\|q_{Q_{i:}})
 &\leq L\|P_{i:}-Q_{i:}\|_1 \nonumber\\
 &\leq 2L\sum_{k=1}^K\max_j m_{j,k}.\label{eq:bound}
\end{align}
Indeed, $h=\sum_j(\alpha_j-\beta_j)\log q_j$ has oscillation at most
$L\|\alpha-\beta\|_1$; the log normalizer ratio is in
$[\inf h,\sup h]$. Telescoping gives a row bound of
$\sum_k\|\widetilde W_k-W_k\|_\infty=2\sum_k\max_jm_{j,k}$.
This elementary bound favors neither receiver rule. Unbounded Gaussian
support, mixture truncation, changing labels and intervening observations
prevent its direct use as a guarantee for the recursive tracker.

\section{Experiments and Remaining Tradeoffs}
\subsection{Paired protocol}
M24/X36 have four/six six-sensor formations and 16/24 targets over 160 steps,
with overlapping target handovers and route failures. Formation members
share a heading, $120^\circ$ FoV and 300\,m range. Nominal detection
probability, clutter rate and noise are 0.88, 4 and 7\,m; sensing degrades
with range and off-axis angle. Seed 1301 pairs truth, measurements, directed
delivery uniforms and filter randomness within each scale.

Fixed routing keeps its initial formation tree and recomputes gateways while
feasible; otherwise it retains the still-physical initial sensor edges.
Full repair retains two nonself inputs, and sparse repair uses
(\ref{eq:messages}); both may repair the tree. All three use
(\ref{eq:renorm}). The X36 self-fallback ablation changes only handling of
unavailable or empty neighbor inputs to (\ref{eq:self}).

All arms share approximate powered-GM fusion (three components/input, eight
output components, 256 tuples); overlap above one triggers moment-matched
fallback. Arbitrary GM powers are not exact. Detection probability is
evaluated at component means, without boundary splitting
\cite{legrand2022split}. Present labels are not removed solely for being
outside the FoV. Each step uses one fusion round, shared birth labels without
matching, MAP-cardinality output and existence pruning below 0.01.

Metrics are Euclidean position OSPA (E-OSPA, order 2, cutoff 150\,m)
\cite{schuhmacher2008ospa} and Hungarian-matched position RMSE, averaged over
sensor--time cells. RMSE excludes nonempty--empty comparisons and must be
read with absolute cardinality error. Focus consistency is pairwise E-OSPA
among one fixed representative per formation over $t=40$--140, not all-node
agreement. Attempted posterior bytes include failed transmissions but
exclude routing-control traffic.

Table~\ref{tab:main} and Fig.~\ref{fig:tradeoff} separate repair from
sparsification. Full repair lowers E-OSPA, RMSE and disagreement but costs
more bytes. Sparse repair reduces traffic; on X36 its lower RMSE coexists
with worse count error and disagreement. Table~\ref{tab:guardrails} retains
common-cell and formation-tail checks: localization gains survive the former,
but means do not establish uniform benefits. Self fallback improves X36 set
error and disagreement while worsening common-cell and formation-tail RMSE.

\subsection{How much can localization alone improve?}
To separate position refinement from target-set recovery, write squared
OSPA at one sensor--time cell as
\begin{equation}
 E^2=C+L,\qquad C=c^2\frac{|n-T|}{\max(n,T)},\quad L\geq0,
 \label{eq:countbudget}
\end{equation}
where $T,n$ are true and estimated counts and $c=150$\,m; empty--empty gives
zero. Perfect localization leaves $\sqrt C$. Saved count errors omit sign;
OSPA/RMSE constraints give $C_{\min}\leq C\leq C_{\max}$, with 0/3840 M24
and 121/5760 X36 sparse cells unresolved. Count terms account for 99.567\%
and 98.074--99.337\% of summed squared OSPA, not mean OSPA.
Figure~\ref{fig:tradeoff}(c) gives the fixed-count ceiling
$\operatorname{mean}(E-\sqrt{C_{\min}})$, not an achieved gain. Substantial
improvement requires target-set recovery, not just position refinement.

\section{Conclusion}
Sparse repair saves posterior traffic but retains target-set and formation
tradeoffs. Useful existence evidence and spatial compatibility remain joint
constraints. Multi-view and label-matched baselines, independent seeds and
layouts, control traffic and decentralized routing remain open.

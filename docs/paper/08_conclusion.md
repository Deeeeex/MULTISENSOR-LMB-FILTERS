# Conclusion

## Paper-Ready Conclusion Draft

This paper studies distributed GA-LMB or KLA-based fusion for multi-sensor multi-object tracking under communication constraints. The starting point is that Fixed Metropolis weighting is too rigid for networks in which posterior quality and effective communication quality vary across nodes. In the present peer-to-peer setting, the most useful question is therefore not whether geometric-average fusion should be abandoned, but how its weights should be allocated so that they reflect both local posterior quality and realized network conditions.

The current answer supported by the experiments is a narrow and pragmatic one. The most effective adaptive design is built on three factors: covariance quality, realized link quality, and existence confidence. Covariance captures posterior concentration, link quality captures how reliably a node's information is actually delivered, and existence confidence captures a missing dimension that is not expressed by covariance alone, namely how decisively a local Bernoulli posterior supports target existence. On top of this three-factor backbone, branch decoupling separates the spatial and existence-weight paths. The spatial path keeps the communication-aware backbone, while the existence path can receive the FID-FIA cue that is empirically strongest for cardinality. This gives two recommended operating modes: a Balanced mode for position-sensitive deployment and a Cardinality-critical mode for target-number agreement.

In the dual-formation eight-sensor scenario under tiered heterogeneous packet loss, this design yields clear improvements in distributed consensus quality. Relative to Fixed Metropolis, the Cardinality-critical mode reduces OSPA consensus error from `2.453677` to `1.668961`, matched localization disagreement from `2.335508` to `1.528182`, and cardinality dispersion from `0.714625` to `0.061062` across 20 paired trials. The runtime supplement adds the deployment side of the same result: the Balanced mode is close to fixed-weight cost at about `1.17x` fixed runtime, while FID-FIA-weighted GA and Cardinality-critical mode are about `2.91x` and `3.02x`, respectively. The ablation study further shows that covariance and link quality form the essential backbone, that existence confidence is the most effective third factor, and that the branch-specific refinements provide two useful operating modes: a Balanced mode for low-overhead position-sensitive deployment and a Cardinality-critical mode for higher-cost cardinality-sensitive deployment.

The supporting studies help clarify what this result does and does not mean. Under ideal communication, both operating modes still improve network disagreement metrics relative to Fixed Metropolis, so the gain is not only a packet-loss compensation effect. At the same time, several alternative modules, including plain NIS rewards, freshness weighting, history weighting, and preliminary cardinality-consensus corrections, currently show weak, mixed, or negative evidence. For that reason, they should remain secondary or appendix-only components rather than part of the main claimed method.

The present work therefore supports a focused conclusion: communication-aware adaptive fusion for distributed KLA-based LMB tracking benefits most from combining posterior concentration, realized communication reliability, and existence decisiveness, then decoupling the spatial and existence branches so that FID-FIA contributes where it improves cardinality without becoming a scalar whole-posterior replacement. The Balanced mode should be selected when RMSE/spatial stability or computation budget is the limiting requirement; the Cardinality-critical mode should be selected when target-number agreement is the limiting requirement and roughly three times the fixed-weight filtering/fusion runtime is acceptable. Future work should strengthen this conclusion through larger Monte Carlo studies, cleaner communication-level robustness tables, stronger external baselines, richer heterogeneous sensing scenarios, runtime optimization of the FID-FIA branch, and more principled dynamic structure-consistency models that can be validated without reintroducing excessive coupling into the weight design. A particularly useful extension is asynchronous or multi-rate distributed LMB fusion: the current freshness and availability terms could be replaced by time-aligned posterior propagation and continuous-time Fisher-information decay, while target-wise information weights could be explored once label association across neighboring LMB posteriors is reliable enough.

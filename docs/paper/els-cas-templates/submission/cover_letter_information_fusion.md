Dear Editor,

Please consider our manuscript, "Communication-Aware Adaptive Weights for Consensus-Oriented Distributed KLA-Based LMB Fusion", for publication as a research article in Information Fusion.

The manuscript studies distributed multi-sensor multi-object tracking in peer-to-peer sensor networks where local posterior quality and realized packet delivery vary across sensors. We propose a communication-aware adaptive weighting family for distributed Kullback-Leibler average (KLA), also known as geometric-average, labeled multi-Bernoulli (LMB) fusion. The method combines posterior covariance, realized link quality, and Bernoulli existence confidence, then uses branch-specific refinements: a weak structure-aware spatial weighting path and an optional FID-FIA-informed existence path for cardinality-critical operation.

The paper fits the scope of Information Fusion because it addresses algorithmic multi-sensor fusion under communication constraints, including adaptive fusion architecture, distributed sensor networks, uncertainty-aware fusion rules, and computational cost.

## Required Guide-For-Authors Points

### 1. Comparison with state-of-the-art

Yes. The manuscript compares the proposed method against fixed/topology-derived Metropolis weighting, PD-weighted GA, and FID-FIA-weighted GA as the information-geometric baseline. It also includes factor ablations, ideal-communication support, communication-level sensitivity, local tracking safeguards, and runtime measurements. The most relevant state-of-the-art comparison is the FID-FIA-weighted GA baseline; the proposed Cardinality-critical mode improves over that baseline in the main tiered heterogeneous packet-loss scenario while preserving a communication-aware spatial branch.

### 3. Public datasets

The study is simulation-based and does not use a public real-world dataset. The data are synthetic multi-object tracking scenarios generated by the repository scripts with deterministic seeds. The manuscript reports the seed ranges and stored result logs used for the tables. Before final submission, the authors should provide a public archival repository or DOI for the code, scripts, and generated result reports, or state that they are available from the corresponding author on reasonable request.

### 4. Validation measures

The validation uses standard multi-object tracking measures and deployment-relevant fusion measures. Truth-referenced local safeguards include E-OSPA, local RMSE, and local cardinality error. The primary distributed-fusion outcomes are OSPA consensus error, matched localization disagreement, and cardinality dispersion, built from standard finite-set distance and assignment ingredients to quantify cross-node agreement after distributed fusion. The paper also reports filter/fusion wall-clock runtime, runtime per simulation step, and runtime relative to fixed Metropolis weighting.

### 5. Main claim and significance

The main claim is that distributed KLA-based LMB fusion under heterogeneous communication should not rely on fixed or topology-only weights. Fusion weights should reflect posterior concentration, realized communication reliability, and existence decisiveness, with branch-specific treatment of spatial and existence components. This matters because communication-constrained distributed fusion can otherwise produce sensors that agree poorly about target states and target number even when each node runs a valid local LMB filter.

### 6. Evidence supporting the claim

In the main 20-trial paired tiered heterogeneous packet-loss scenario, Fixed Metropolis gives OSPA consensus error, matched localization disagreement, and cardinality dispersion of 2.454, 2.336, and 0.715. The proposed Cardinality-critical mode reduces these to 1.669, 1.528, and 0.061, respectively. The lower-overhead Balanced mode gives a position-oriented alternative with substantially lower runtime than the information-geometric cardinality-critical option. The paper also includes local tracking safeguards showing that the consensus gains are not produced by local tracking collapse, a communication-level sensitivity probe from levels 0 to 3, and a runtime supplement showing the computational tradeoff between Balanced and Cardinality-critical operation.

### 7. Most related work and difference

The closest adaptive-weight precedent is the information-geometric FID-FIA-style distributed heterogeneous labeled-RFS fusion line. The present manuscript differs by staying in peer-to-peer distributed GA/KLA-based LMB fusion, adding realized packet-delivery information to the weight backbone, and applying FID-FIA only to the Bernoulli existence branch instead of using a single scalar information-geometric weight for the whole fused posterior. The paper also differs from resilient peer-to-peer LMB fusion and distributed MB/GCI fusion by focusing on adaptive weight allocation under heterogeneous packet loss rather than only the underlying conservative fusion operator.

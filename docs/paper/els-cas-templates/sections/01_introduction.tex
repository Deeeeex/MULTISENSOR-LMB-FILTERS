\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Distributed multisensor, multi-object tracking is central to surveillance, autonomous systems, and cooperative perception. We study peer-to-peer tracking with partial field-of-view (FOV) coverage and changing posterior quality. Communication is constrained by bandwidth limits, packet drops, and temporary outages \cite{Zhang2016CommConstrainedFusion}. Random finite sets (RFSs), finite-set statistics (FISST), and labeled-RFS filters represent target number, kinematic state, and identity within a Bayesian framework \cite{Mahler2003PHD,VoMa2006GMPHD,Mahler2007FISSTBook,Vo2014LRFS,Reuter2014LMB}. The remaining question is how to combine neighboring labeled posteriors when sensing geometry, posterior uncertainty, and packet delivery vary across the network.

Within this family, generalized labeled multi-Bernoulli (GLMB) and labeled multi-Bernoulli (LMB) filters balance modeling fidelity against computational cost \cite{Vo2014LRFS,Reuter2014LMB,Vo2019MSGLMB,Mahler2019ExactBayes,Vo2024OverviewLRFS}. For distributed fusion, the geometric-average (GA) realization of Kullback--Leibler averaging (KLA) provides a conservative logarithmic opinion pool. This rule, also known as generalized covariance intersection (GCI), is useful when unknown cross-correlations preclude exact Bayesian fusion \cite{JulierUhlmann1997CI,Mahler2000RobustFusion,Battistelli2014KLA,Hlinka2014ICI,Uney2013EMDPHD,Bandyopadhyay2018LogOP}. GA/KLA-based LMB fusion is therefore a natural peer-to-peer backbone. The unresolved design question is how to allocate its weights online.

Fixed Metropolis, uniform, or degree-based consensus weights are easy to deploy \cite{XiaoBoydLall2005ConsensusWeights}. They cannot distinguish precise from ambiguous posteriors, nor can they represent the delivery quality realized on each link. A single adaptive score is also too coarse for an LMB posterior. A Bernoulli component may be spatially sharp but uncertain in existence, or decisive in existence but spatially diffuse. A factorized rule is therefore needed to allocate separate branch weights under communication constraints.

This paper addresses that gap with communication-aware weight allocation for distributed GA-LMB fusion. Figure~\ref{fig:system-overview} summarizes the update at one peer. The design combines covariance quality, realized link quality, and existence confidence in a shared backbone. It then separates the spatial and existence branches of the LMB fusion rule. A weak graph prior regularizes spatial fusion while remaining secondary to posterior quality. An FID-FIA refinement, adapted from Cao and Zhao \cite{CaoZhao2025InfoGeometryFusion}, acts only on Bernoulli existence. This design yields Balanced mode for localization-sensitive use and Cardinality-critical mode for target-number agreement.

We evaluate the method in an eight-sensor formation scenario with variation induced by geometry and FOV, together with heterogeneous packet delivery. Network disagreement is the primary outcome because the method targets agreement in the fused multisensor picture. Truth-referenced local metrics and filter runtime guard against hidden losses in accuracy or deployability. We also compare against explicitly labeled GA-LMB adaptations of two related methods whose original filter interfaces or threat models differ from ours. The results expose the intended tradeoff. The three-factor backbone provides most of the low-cost gain, whereas FID-FIA is most useful when cardinality agreement is the dominant risk.

The main contributions of this paper are as follows.
\begin{enumerate}
\item A communication-aware reliability backbone for adaptive GA/KLA-LMB fusion weights under heterogeneous packet delivery.
\item A branch-decoupled LMB fusion-weight rule that separates spatial and existence weighting and confines FID-FIA to the Bernoulli existence branch.
\item A consensus-centered evaluation under packet loss and ideal links, including four communication levels, runtime measurements, GOSPA, and two literature-method adaptations.
\end{enumerate}

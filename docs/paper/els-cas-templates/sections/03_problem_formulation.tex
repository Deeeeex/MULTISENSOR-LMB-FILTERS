\section{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:problem-formulation}

Consider a distributed sensor network with sensor index set $\mathcal{V}=\{1,\ldots,S\}$, where $S$ is the number of sensors, operating over discrete time $k \in \{1,\ldots,T\}$. The multi-object state at time $k$ is modeled as a labeled random finite set $X_k$, and each sensor $s \in \mathcal{V}$ acquires a local measurement set $Z_k^{(s)}$. Following the standard RFS/FISST and labeled-RFS formulation, each node maintains an LMB approximation to the local multi-object posterior, whose Bernoulli components jointly encode target existence, kinematic state, and identity \cite{Mahler2003PHD,Mahler2007FISSTBook,Vo2014LRFS,Reuter2014LMB,Vo2019MSGLMB}. The objective of the distributed fusion layer is to combine neighboring local posteriors into a consensus-quality posterior without assuming that cross-correlations among nodes are known.

\subsection{Local LMB posterior model}

Let $\mathcal{N}_s \subseteq \mathcal{V}$ denote the communication neighborhood of sensor $s$, including the node itself. In the setting considered here, $\mathcal{N}_s$ is induced by a communication graph derived from sensor geometry and communication range, and each node performs local fusion only over the posteriors available inside this neighborhood. After local prediction, measurement update, and data association, node $j \in \mathcal{N}_s$ produces a measurement-updated LMB posterior. We use $\Theta_k^{(j)}$ for its finite parameter set,
\begin{equation}
\Theta_{k}^{(j)} = \left\{ \left(r_{k,i}^{(j)}, p_{k,i}^{(j)}(\cdot,\ell_i)\right) \right\}_{i=1}^{M_k},
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{L}_k=\{\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_{M_k}\}$ is the aligned label set at time $k$, $M_k$ is the number of aligned Bernoulli labels, $r_{k,i}^{(j)} \in [0,1]$ is the Bernoulli existence probability, and $p_{k,i}^{(j)}$ is the associated spatial density for label $\ell_i$. The corresponding LMB density is not this parameter set itself; it is the standard labeled-RFS density induced by the parameter set,
\begin{equation}
\pi_k^{(j)}(X)
=
\Delta(X)\,
w_k^{(j)}\!\left(\mathcal{L}(X)\right)
\prod_{(x,\ell_i)\in X}p_{k,i}^{(j)}(x,\ell_i),
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
w_k^{(j)}(L)
=
\prod_{\ell_i\in L}r_{k,i}^{(j)}
\prod_{\ell_i\in \mathcal{L}_k\setminus L}\left(1-r_{k,i}^{(j)}\right),
\qquad
L\subseteq\mathcal{L}_k.
\end{equation}
Here $\Delta(X)$ is the distinct-label indicator and $\mathcal{L}(X)$ is the set of labels present in $X$. The compact parameter-set notation $\Theta_k^{(j)}$ is used below whenever the implementation operates on the Bernoulli components rather than on the full finite-set density.

\subsection{Communication-constrained delivery}

Before local measurements are used by a neighboring fusion node, they pass through a communication layer with three possible effects:
\begin{enumerate}
\item a global or per-step bandwidth budget that limits how many measurements can be delivered,
\item a stochastic packet-drop model on each sensor link,
\item optional node-level outages.
\end{enumerate}
Accordingly, the posterior that reaches a neighbor reflects both the underlying sensing quality and the measurements that were actually delivered. Let $m_k^{(j)} \in \{0,1\}$ denote the availability indicator of node $j$ at time $k$, and let $\rho_k^{(j)} \in [0,1]$ denote the realized link-quality statistic derived from delivered-versus-dropped packets.

\subsection{Distributed KLA fusion objective}

Because the cross-correlation structure among neighboring LMB posteriors is unknown, exact Bayesian fusion is generally unavailable. We therefore adopt the geometric-average realization of Kullback-Leibler averaging, also known as generalized covariance intersection, as the main conservative fusion rule. At node $s$, the target fused density over the neighborhood $\mathcal{N}_s$ is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ga-kla-objective}
\bar{\pi}_k^{(s)}(X) \propto \prod_{j \in \mathcal{N}_s} \left(\pi_k^{(j)}(X)\right)^{\omega_{k,s}^{(j)}},
\qquad
\sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}_s} \omega_{k,s}^{(j)} = 1,
\qquad
\omega_{k,s}^{(j)} \ge 0,
\end{equation}
where $\omega_{k,s}^{(j)}$ is the fusion weight assigned by node $s$ to neighbor $j$ at time $k$ \cite{Battistelli2014KLA,Hlinka2014ICI}. Some average-fusion literature uses KL-averaging terminology more broadly. To avoid ambiguity, unless stated otherwise, the shorthand KLA/GA in this paper refers to the geometric-average/logarithmic-pool solution in \eqref{eq:ga-kla-objective}; the appendix-only AA diagnostics are discussed separately as arithmetic-average density fusion along the reverse-KL/minimum-information-loss route \cite{LiCorchadoSun2017GCIAlternative,Gao2020MIL,Li2024AADensityFusion}. With fixed-weight fusion, $\omega_{k,s}^{(j)}$ is often chosen as a uniform or Metropolis-type consensus weight \cite{XiaoBoydLall2005ConsensusWeights}. The central problem studied here is how to choose these weights adaptively so that they reflect both posterior quality and communication quality.

In the GA-LMB fusion rule considered here, the spatial and existence parts of each Bernoulli component are fused separately after moment matching. This separation makes it natural to ask whether the same scalar weight should govern both spatial fusion and existence fusion.

\subsection{Adaptive weight-allocation target}

The adaptive layer must output active-neighbor weight vectors on the simplex. In the fixed-weight case, the same vector $\omega_{k,s}^{(j)}$ can be used for the whole posterior. In the branch-decoupled case studied here, the output is a spatial vector $\omega_{k,s}^{x,(j)}$ and an existence vector $\omega_{k,s}^{r,(j)}$. Both are normalized over the available neighborhood, but they are allowed to respond to different reliability cues because the Gaussian spatial update and the Bernoulli existence update consume the weights in different algebraic blocks. Section~\ref{sec:method} gives the concrete score definitions and the per-node algorithmic flow; this section only states the fusion target and the quantities that the adaptive layer must produce.

\subsection{Evaluation target}

The aim of the above formulation is to improve each node's local tracking output and agreement among nodes. This distinction is central in the present problem. Under unknown cross-correlations and communication losses, distributed fusion should move neighboring nodes toward a shared multi-sensor picture.

Accordingly, the evaluation uses two complementary metric layers:
\begin{itemize}
\item primary network-level disagreement metrics, which quantify consensus error among the fused outputs of different sensors,
\item secondary truth-referenced local tracking metrics, which verify that consensus-error reductions preserve conventional tracking accuracy.
\end{itemize}

Let $\hat{X}_k^{(s)}$ denote the extracted multi-object estimate at sensor $s$ and time $k$, and let $\hat{n}_k^{(s)} = |\hat{X}_k^{(s)}|$ denote its estimated cardinality. The paper defines the following network-level disagreement metrics:
\begin{align}
D_{\mathrm{OSPA}}(k)
&=
\frac{2}{S(S-1)}
\sum_{1 \le a < b \le S}
d_{\mathrm{OSPA}}\!\left(\hat{X}_k^{(a)}, \hat{X}_k^{(b)}\right),
\\
D_{\mathrm{loc}}(k)
&=
\frac{2}{S(S-1)}
\sum_{1 \le a < b \le S}
d_{\mathrm{loc}}\!\left(\hat{X}_k^{(a)}, \hat{X}_k^{(b)}\right),
\\
D_{\mathrm{card}}(k)
&=
\frac{1}{S}
\sum_{s=1}^{S}
\left|
\hat{n}_k^{(s)}
-
\operatorname{median}\!\left(\hat{n}_k^{(1)},\ldots,\hat{n}_k^{(S)}\right)
\right|,
\end{align}
where $d_{\mathrm{OSPA}}$ is the pairwise optimal subpattern assignment (OSPA) distance between two estimated finite sets and $d_{\mathrm{loc}}$ is the matched localization disagreement obtained after Hungarian matching on the common extracted objects \cite{Schuhmacher2008OSPA,Ristic2011TrackMetric,Kuhn1955Hungarian}. In the paper these are reported as OSPA consensus error, matched localization disagreement, and cardinality dispersion, respectively.

These three quantities are task-driven network-level disagreement measures built from standard ingredients: OSPA and related generalized OSPA (GOSPA)-style distances are mainstream finite-set tracking metrics, Hungarian matching is the standard assignment primitive behind many tracking scores, and pairwise dispersion is the usual way to quantify disagreement in consensus problems \cite{Schuhmacher2008OSPA,Rahmathullah2017GOSPA,Ristic2011TrackMetric,XiaoBoydLall2005ConsensusWeights}. What is specific to this paper is the aggregation target: in addition to comparing each sensor with ground truth, the metrics compare the post-fusion outputs of different nodes. They are introduced because the present problem is distributed fusion under heterogeneous communication, where the main question is whether neighboring nodes move toward a common fused picture. The paper therefore reports these network-level disagreement metrics together with the secondary truth-referenced local metrics listed below.

The secondary local metrics remain the standard truth-referenced tracking quantities:
\begin{itemize}
\item local expected OSPA (E-OSPA),
\item local root mean square error (RMSE),
\item local cardinality error.
\end{itemize}
The evaluation logic is therefore asymmetric by design: the method is primarily successful if it reduces network-level disagreement, while the local metrics are used as a safeguard to show that this gain is not purchased by a clear loss in conventional tracking quality.

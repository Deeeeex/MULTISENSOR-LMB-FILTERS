\section{Results and Discussion}
\label{sec:results}

This section reports the empirical evidence supporting the paper's main claims. The strongest evidence comes from the tiered heterogeneous packet-loss GA scenario introduced in Section~\ref{sec:experimental-setup}, so that scenario is treated as the primary result. Ideal communication and communication-sensitivity experiments are used as supporting studies. Appendix-only results document exploratory variants.

\subsection{Main comparison and design ablation}

\paragraph{Consensus and local safeguards.}
We first tested whether communication-aware weighting improved agreement in the eight-sensor GA-LMB scenario with tiered packet loss. Network disagreement is reported before truth-referenced local accuracy because agreement is the method's primary target. The comparison addresses three questions. First, can a direct reliability weight improve GA fusion? Second, does separation of the spatial and existence branches add value? Third, does an existence-specific Fisher cue help when cardinality errors dominate? The probability-of-detection (PD)-weighted GA row addresses the first question by weighting each target with its local detection probability.

Table~\ref{tab:main-consensus-stats} shows the progression across method variants. PD-weighted GA improved on Fixed Metropolis, so direct reliability weighting was already useful under heterogeneous delivery. Scalar FID-FIA weighting was particularly effective for cardinality dispersion. The proposed modes gave the stronger overall balance. Balanced mode achieved the lowest localization disagreement. Cardinality-critical mode achieved the lowest OSPA and GOSPA consensus errors and cardinality dispersion. Relative to Fixed Metropolis, its OSPA, localization, and cardinality metrics fell from $2.469$ to $1.678$, from $2.326$ to $1.546$, and from $0.716$ to $0.063$. These changes correspond to reductions of $32.1\%$, $33.5\%$, and $91.2\%$. Its unnormalized GOSPA error fell from $4.780$ to $3.904$, an $18.3\%$ reduction. For each proposed mode, all 50 paired trials improved on Fixed Metropolis for all four network metrics. The exact two-sided sign-test value was $p=1.8\times10^{-15}$ in each comparison. Figure~\ref{fig:main-consensus} shows unsmoothed, 50-trial means of three temporal trajectories. Both modes remained below Fixed Metropolis for most time steps, with the clearest cardinality separation in Cardinality-critical mode.

Table~\ref{tab:main-local} checks whether the consensus gains conceal a loss in local tracking accuracy. PD-weighted GA achieved the lowest truth-referenced RMSE, but it was weaker in E-OSPA and local cardinality error. Cardinality-critical mode showed the complementary pattern. It achieved the best E-OSPA and cardinality error, but its RMSE exceeded those of PD-weighted GA, Fixed Metropolis, and Balanced mode. Its RMSE nevertheless remained slightly below that of scalar FID-FIA weighting. The reduction in cardinality dispersion therefore coincided with better target-count estimates. This pattern is consistent with applying Fisher information only to Bernoulli existence while retaining the communication-aware spatial branch.

The two proposed operating-mode rows are recommended settings of the same method family. The Balanced mode is preferable when spatial RMSE stability is the dominant requirement. The Cardinality-critical mode is preferable when target-number agreement and truth-referenced cardinality error are the dominant risks. This usage-oriented distinction makes the tradeoff explicit.

\paragraph{Reviewer-requested literature adaptations.}
We next tested whether the gains remained under the two literature-motivated adaptations defined in Section~\ref{sec:experimental-setup}. The Zheng-style row preserves subdensity-level branch weighting but maps the source GM-PHD consensus/flooding formulation to label-associated Bernoulli components in our one-round GA-LMB protocol. The Gao-style row preserves receiver-local trust and distance-based attenuation. Its hard rejection is disabled because this experiment contains packet loss but no corrupted posterior parameters. These rows therefore test the portability of the published weighting principles, not exact source-algorithm reproductions.

Table~\ref{tab:reviewer-baselines} shows that neither adaptation transferred favorably to this communication-limited one-round protocol. Balanced mode had lower OSPA, GOSPA, localization disagreement, and cardinality dispersion than each adaptation in all 50 paired trials. Cardinality-critical mode improved on both adaptations in all paired comparisons except GOSPA against the Zheng-style adaptation, where it improved in 49 of 50 trials. The exact two-sided sign-test values were $p=1.8\times10^{-15}$ for the 50-of-50 comparisons and $p=9.1\times10^{-14}$ for the 49-of-50 comparison. The lower local E-OSPA, RMSE, and cardinality-error means in the proposed rows indicate that the network agreement was not obtained by forcing inaccurate local estimates into consensus.

The failure modes are informative. The Zheng-style adaptation uses posterior concentration and existence margin but no realized delivery-quality cue. Under partial FOV and packet loss, a concentrated component can still be poorly aligned with complementary neighbors, which coincided with high localization disagreement. The Gao-style adaptation measures each neighbor against the receiver's local posterior. In an honest network with legitimate cross-view differences, this local-trust prior can attenuate useful complementary evidence even though no attack is present. The resulting cardinality dispersion and GOSPA were high. These findings are specific to the stated adaptations and protocol. They do not establish superiority over the source methods in their native GM-PHD consensus/flooding or adversarial-LMB settings.

Computational cost is the third evaluation axis for this method family. Table~\ref{tab:runtime-cost} reports 50-trial runtime measurements in the same 100-step tiered-drop scenario and covers all arms in the main comparison. The measurement includes only the distributed LMB filtering/fusion call for each arm; scenario generation, communication-model sampling, plotting, and metric evaluation are excluded. The main cost distinction is clear. The Balanced mode remains close to the fixed-weight baseline, requiring about $1.08\times$ the Fixed Metropolis runtime. The PD-weighted GA baseline is also a moderate-cost dynamic-weighting alternative at about $1.23\times$ fixed runtime. By contrast, FID-FIA-weighted GA and the Cardinality-critical mode are both near the three-times-cost regime. The runtime evidence identifies FID-FIA scoring as the dominant source of additional cost.

The cost results sharpen the operating-mode recommendation. The Balanced mode is the low-overhead default when spatial consensus, RMSE stability, runtime, or energy budget is the limiting requirement. The Cardinality-critical mode should be selected when target-number agreement and suppression of missed or false support justify roughly three times the fixed-weight filtering/fusion time.

\paragraph{Factor ablation.}

The most important diagnostic ablation is the factor-by-factor path from Fixed Metropolis to the Balanced mode. Table~\ref{tab:ablation} summarizes this 50-trial path and adds the Cardinality-critical mode as the last row. The three-factor adaptive backbone is omitted as a separate row to keep the table focused on the main operating points. It remains the reliability model used by the branch-decoupled Balanced mode. The last row is the branch-specific existence refinement that converts the Balanced mode into the cardinality-sensitive mode.

Several conclusions follow from Table~\ref{tab:ablation}. First, covariance weighting is necessary but not sufficient. It improves over fixed topology weights and confirms posterior concentration as a useful first-order proxy for local posterior quality. A substantial gap to the branch-decoupled modes nevertheless remains.

Second, realized link quality is the dominant communication-aware factor under heterogeneous packet loss. Adding it after covariance produces the largest incremental change in the backbone path, especially for cardinality dispersion, which is approximately halved. This is the key evidence that the method is not merely reweighting sharper posteriors; it is also responding to whether those posteriors are reliably delivered to neighboring nodes.

Third, existence confidence remains part of the adaptive backbone, and its effect is folded into the branch-decoupled Balanced mode in Table~\ref{tab:ablation}. This is a methodological choice: the reliability model is applied differently to the spatial and Bernoulli existence paths. This is consistent with the design rationale, because covariance and link quality do not directly express how decisive a local Bernoulli component is about target existence.

Finally, the Balanced mode provides the pre-FID-FIA branch-decoupled operating point. The final row applies the FID-FIA cue only to the existence branch. Cardinality dispersion decreases from about $0.188$ to $0.063$, and OSPA consensus error also decreases. This is why the proposed method is presented as a configurable branch-decoupled family: the Balanced mode is the spatial/position-oriented setting, and the Cardinality-critical mode is the cardinality-oriented setting.

The ideal-communication result mirrors the branch specialization observed in the tiered-drop experiment. The PD-weighted GA row confirms that a simple reliability-weighted GA baseline can also help when link degradation is removed. Information-geometric weighting is especially effective for cardinality dispersion. The localization-oriented Balanced mode gives the lowest OSPA consensus error, matched localization disagreement, and local RMSE in this benign setting. The Cardinality-critical mode gives the lowest cardinality dispersion and local E-OSPA without forcing a single FID-FIA weight onto the whole posterior. Thus, the refinement is not merely repairing packet loss, and its consensus gain does not coincide with collapsed local tracking quality.

\paragraph{Communication sensitivity and robustness.}

We next tested Balanced mode across four communication levels. Each level used the same eight-sensor scenario and 50 deterministic paired trials. Level $0$ used ideal communication. Level $1$ added only the global bandwidth cap, and level $2$ added the tiered link-loss model. Level $3$ further added random node outages. Every level used seeds $2$--$51$. The level-$2$ Fixed and Balanced trials are exactly those summarized in Table~\ref{tab:main-consensus-stats}. Table~\ref{tab:comm-level-sensitivity} reports the disagreement metrics, and Figure~\ref{fig:comm-level-sensitivity} adds 95\% confidence intervals. This study varies communication only; it does not vary sensor detection probability or measurement noise.

Balanced mode improved all three disagreement metrics at every communication level. OSPA and localization improved in all 50 trials at each level. Cardinality dispersion improved in 49 of 50 trials at levels $0$ and $1$, and in all trials at levels $2$ and $3$. The exact sign-test values were at most $9.1\times10^{-14}$. Gains were modest under ideal communication or the bandwidth cap alone. They became much larger when link loss or outages created heterogeneous realized delivery. Communication-aware weighting was therefore most useful when link outcomes differed across nodes. We restricted this study to two arms to preserve one coherent protocol. Sensitivity of the more expensive FID-FIA refinement beyond levels $0$ and $2$ remains future work.

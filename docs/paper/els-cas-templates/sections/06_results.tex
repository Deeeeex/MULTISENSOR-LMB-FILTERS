\section{Results and Discussion}
\label{sec:results}

This section separates two kinds of evidence. Tables~\ref{tab:main-consensus-stats}--\ref{tab:runtime-cost} compare complete operating points against fixed and direct adaptive baselines. Table~\ref{tab:ablation} is the controlled component study used for factor attribution. All reported accuracy studies use 50 deterministic trials with seeds $2$--$51$ in the same tiered heterogeneous packet-loss scenario. The direct PD- and FID-FIA-weighted baselines come from dedicated matched-seed probes, so incremental paired claims are reserved for Table~\ref{tab:ablation}.

\subsection{Main result in the tiered-drop GA scenario}

Table~\ref{tab:main-consensus-stats} is the main method-level comparison. PD-weighted GA tests whether a direct sensor-reliability weight is sufficient, while FID-FIA-weighted GA applies one information-geometric weight to the complete posterior. The two proposed rows instead use branch-aware communication-adaptive fusion. Balanced gives the lowest OSPA consensus error and matched localization disagreement. Cardinality-critical adds FID-FIA only to the existence branch and gives the lowest cardinality dispersion.

Relative to Fixed Metropolis, the Balanced mode reduces OSPA consensus error by $28.8\%$, matched localization disagreement by $35.5\%$, and cardinality dispersion by $85.4\%$. It also improves the two spatial disagreement metrics over both direct baselines. Cardinality-critical reduces cardinality dispersion by a further $34.8\%$ relative to Balanced, but its OSPA consensus error increases by $1.0\%$ and localization disagreement by $8.9\%$. The method-level table therefore exposes a spatial-versus-cardinality operating tradeoff rather than a uniformly dominant setting.

Table~\ref{tab:main-local} checks whether stronger agreement is obtained by driving all nodes toward an inaccurate common estimate. PD-weighted GA has the lowest local RMSE, but remains weak on finite-set and cardinality errors. Balanced provides the stronger spatial-consensus operating point. Cardinality-critical improves local E-OSPA and reduces local cardinality error by $26.2\%$ relative to Balanced, but increases local RMSE by $6.6\%$. This mirrors the network-level spatial-versus-cardinality tradeoff.

Computational cost is the third evaluation axis for this method family. Table~\ref{tab:runtime-cost} reports 50-trial runtime measurements in the same 100-step tiered-drop scenario and covers all arms in the main comparison. The measurement includes only the distributed LMB filtering/fusion call for each arm; scenario generation, communication-model sampling, plotting, and metric evaluation are excluded. The main cost distinction is clear. The Balanced mode remains close to the fixed-weight baseline, requiring about $1.08\times$ the Fixed Metropolis runtime. The PD-weighted GA baseline is also a moderate-cost dynamic-weighting alternative at about $1.23\times$ fixed runtime. By contrast, FID-FIA-weighted GA and the Cardinality-critical mode are both near the three-times-cost regime. The runtime evidence therefore identifies FID-FIA scoring, not the basic communication-aware weighting backbone or the direct PD-weighted baseline, as the dominant source of additional cost.

The cost results sharpen the operating-mode recommendation. The Balanced mode is the low-overhead default when spatial consensus, RMSE stability, runtime, or energy budget is the limiting requirement. The Cardinality-critical mode should be selected when target-number agreement and suppression of missed or false support justify roughly three times the fixed-weight filtering/fusion time.

The detailed results support five bounded conclusions. First, realized link quality is the dominant factor in this packet-loss scenario. Second, covariance provides a useful independent signal and gives a further cardinality benefit when combined with link quality. Third, existence confidence has little isolated incremental effect on the covariance-link backbone. Fourth, the combined branch-aware refinement supplies a small, repeatable spatial benefit. Fifth, existence-only FID-FIA provides a substantial additional cardinality benefit but does not improve the spatial disagreement metrics. The table intentionally does not separate branch decoupling from its weak structure-aware modulation.

\subsection{Scope of the revised evidence}

The Balanced and Cardinality-critical configurations are confirmed here in the main tiered heterogeneous packet-loss scenario. Earlier ideal-communication, communication-level, and arithmetic-average probes predate the current operating modes and are therefore not used as supporting evidence. Repeating those studies with the current configuration remains a targeted validation step.

\subsection{Payload semantics in a high-clutter crossing stress}
\label{sec:payload-semantics}

Table~\ref{tab:mixture-heavy-stress} and Figure~\ref{fig:payload-semantics-stress} report the separate payload-semantics stress described in Section~\ref{sec:payload-stress-protocol}. The first two rows are intentionally identical in tracking metrics: under the legacy moment-matched KLA path, full heavy GM-LMB messages are compressed to the same label-wise moments before fusion, so their larger payload does not translate into a richer fused posterior. This result is a useful negative control. It shows that a communication protocol cannot justify heavy messages solely by sending a larger posterior object; the receiver-side fusion operator must preserve the information that makes the heavy message different.

The mixture-aware heavy row changes this outcome. It reduces full-horizon mean E-OSPA by $3.5\%$ relative to light, reduces crossing-window mean E-OSPA by $4.3\%$, and improves the absolute crossing-window p90 and worst-case values. It also wins the paired crossing-window comparison in $16$ of the $20$ seeds. The same result is not a blanket claim that heavy payloads are always preferable: the mixture-aware arm transmits substantially more data than light summaries, and its per-seed crossing percent-change p90 remains slightly positive. The supported conclusion is narrower and more useful for this paper: heavy payloads matter only when the fusion operator is allowed to keep selected multi-modal spatial information, and even then the implementation needs source-level gating and a fused-dominance fallback to avoid propagating unresolved mixture ambiguity.

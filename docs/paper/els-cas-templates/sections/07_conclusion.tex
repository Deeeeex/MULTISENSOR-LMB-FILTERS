\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper studies distributed KLA-based (geometric-average) LMB fusion for multi-sensor multi-object tracking under communication constraints. The starting point is that fixed fusion weights are too rigid for networks in which posterior quality and effective communication quality vary across nodes. In the present peer-to-peer setting, the most useful question is therefore not whether geometric-average fusion should be abandoned, but how its weights should be allocated so that they reflect both local posterior quality and realized network conditions.

The experiments support a focused conclusion. The most effective adaptive design is built on three factors: covariance quality, realized link quality, and existence confidence. Covariance captures posterior concentration, link quality captures how reliably a node's information is actually delivered, and existence confidence captures a missing dimension that is not expressed by covariance alone, namely how decisively a local Bernoulli posterior supports target existence. On top of this three-factor backbone, a weak structure-aware decoupled KLA refinement provides a spatial-consensus improvement, and an FID-FIA-informed existence refinement provides the final cardinality-consensus correction. These refinements act as branch-specific corrections rather than as topology-dominated or information-geometry-only replacements for the core adaptive weighting rule.

In the dual-formation eight-sensor scenario under tiered heterogeneous packet loss, this design yields clear improvements in distributed consensus quality. Relative to fixed Metropolis weights, the full adaptive method reduces consensus OSPA from $2.453677$ to $1.668961$, consensus RMSE from $2.335508$ to $1.528182$, and consensus cardinality disagreement from $0.714625$ to $0.061062$ across 20 paired trials. At the same time, the conventional local tracking metrics do not collapse in the main scenario: mean local E-OSPA decreases from $2.853096$ to $2.009084$, local H-OSPA remains essentially unchanged at $0.500$, and mean local cardinality error decreases from $1.454500$ to $0.221563$. Mean local RMSE is $1.704538$, which is worse than the pre-refinement structure-aware arm but slightly better than the Cao-Zhao FID-FIA baseline. The comparison against the Cao-Zhao FID-FIA baseline shows that information-geometry weighting is especially strong for cardinality, and that restricting it to the existence branch can preserve spatial consensus while further reducing both consensus cardinality disagreement and truth-referenced local cardinality error. The ablation study further shows that covariance and link quality form the essential backbone, that existence confidence is the most effective third factor, and that the branch-specific refinements provide the final improvements beyond the three-factor baseline.

The supporting studies help clarify what this result does and does not mean. Under ideal communication, the structure-aware decoupled refinement still improves both consensus metrics and aggregate local tracking metrics relative to ordinary distributed GA, so its gain is not only a packet-loss compensation effect. At the same time, several alternative modules, including plain NIS rewards, freshness weighting, history weighting, and preliminary cardinality-consensus corrections, show weak, mixed, or negative evidence. For that reason, they should remain secondary or appendix-only components rather than part of the main claimed method.

The present work therefore supports a focused conclusion: communication-aware adaptive fusion for distributed KLA-based LMB tracking benefits most from combining posterior concentration, realized communication reliability, and existence decisiveness, then applying branch-specific refinements that reserve information-geometry scoring for existence/cardinality fusion. Future work should strengthen this conclusion through larger Monte Carlo studies, cleaner communication-level robustness tables, stronger external baselines, richer heterogeneous sensing scenarios, and more principled dynamic structure-consistency models that can be validated without reintroducing excessive coupling into the weight design.

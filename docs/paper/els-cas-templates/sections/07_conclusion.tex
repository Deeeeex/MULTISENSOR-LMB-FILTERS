\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper studies communication-aware weights for distributed GA/KLA fusion of LMB posteriors under heterogeneous packet delivery. Fixed topology weights cannot respond when posterior concentration, Bernoulli existence confidence, and realized link quality differ across nodes. The proposed three-factor backbone adapts the fusion weights at low cost. Separate spatial and existence branches allow each part of the LMB posterior to use the relevant reliability cues.

Both proposed modes improved network disagreement in the eight-sensor tiered-loss scenario. Balanced mode kept runtime close to fixed weighting and gave the strongest localization result. Cardinality-critical mode applied FID-FIA only to existence and gave the strongest target-number agreement. Its runtime was comparable to other FID-FIA methods. Both modes also outperformed two explicitly labeled literature-method adaptations on paired network metrics. The ideal-communication study showed that these effects were not limited to packet-loss compensation. Across four communication levels, Balanced mode produced its largest gains when realized delivery became heterogeneous.

The evidence has clear limits. Geometry and FOV vary across sensors, and communication is heterogeneous. Detection probability, clutter rate, and measurement-noise scale remain homogeneous. The experiments also assume honest nodes and Gaussian measurement errors. The literature rows adapt weighting principles to our one-round GA-LMB interface; they are not exact reproductions of the source GM-PHD propagation scheme or adversarial LMB detector. Robustness to packet loss must therefore not be interpreted as robustness to impulsive noise or Byzantine reports. Future work should examine robust scatter estimates, bounded innovation scores, and credibility limits under explicit contamination and attack models. Other priorities include native-protocol comparisons, richer sensing heterogeneity, larger Monte Carlo studies, cheaper FID-FIA implementations, and asynchronous or multirate LMB fusion.

\begin{abstract}
Distributed multisensor, multi-object tracking over peer-to-peer networks requires conservative fusion because cross-correlations are unknown. Fixed consensus weights, however, cannot reflect changing posterior quality or the measurements actually delivered. This paper introduces a communication-aware weighting rule for the geometric-average (GA) realization of Kullback--Leibler average (KLA) fusion of labeled multi-Bernoulli (LMB) posteriors. Each node evaluates available neighbors using posterior concentration, realized link quality computed from delivery outcomes, and Bernoulli existence confidence. Separate spatial and existence branches then use these cues according to their fusion roles. Covariance and link quality govern Gaussian spatial fusion, whereas existence confidence governs Bernoulli support. An optional Fisher information distance and Fisher information accumulation (FID-FIA) cue acts only on the existence branch. The resulting Balanced mode uses the low-overhead branch backbone. Cardinality-critical mode activates the FID-FIA refinement when target-number agreement is the main risk. In an eight-sensor scenario with finite fields of view and heterogeneous packet loss, both modes reduce network disagreement relative to Fixed Metropolis and the internal adaptive baselines. They also outperform two explicitly labeled GA-LMB adaptations of related methods. Truth-referenced local accuracy is retained as a safeguard. These results frame adaptive GA-LMB fusion as a tradeoff among spatial agreement, cardinality agreement, and computation.
\end{abstract}

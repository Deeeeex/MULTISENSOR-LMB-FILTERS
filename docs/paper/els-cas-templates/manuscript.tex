\begin{abstract}
Distributed multi-sensor multi-object tracking over peer-to-peer networks requires fusion rules that remain reliable when local posteriors are heterogeneous and communication links are imperfect. Kullback-Leibler average (KLA), or geometric-average fusion, is attractive because it remains conservative under unknown cross-correlations, yet fixed or topology-derived weights cannot reflect time-varying posterior quality or realized link reliability. This paper proposes a communication-aware adaptive fusion-weight allocation method for distributed GA-LMB fusion under packet loss. The proposed weighting rule combines posterior covariance quality, realized link quality, and an existence-confidence term that measures how decisively a local Bernoulli posterior supports target existence. A weak structure-aware decoupled KLA refinement is then introduced to modulate the spatial and existence branches without letting topology dominate the fusion rule. Because the goal is to improve network-level agreement rather than only single-node accuracy, the experiments evaluate both consensus metrics and conventional local tracking metrics. In an eight-sensor distributed formation scenario composed of two four-sensor formations under tiered heterogeneous packet loss, the method reduces consensus OSPA, consensus RMSE, and consensus cardinality disagreement from $2.624$, $2.703$, and $0.879$ to $1.862$, $1.750$, and $0.244$, respectively, while mean local E-OSPA and local cardinality error also improve and local RMSE remains essentially unchanged. These results show that communication-aware adaptive weighting is an effective way to improve distributed KLA-based LMB fusion in heterogeneous network conditions without sacrificing conventional tracking quality.
\end{abstract}

\begin{highlights}
\item Communication-aware adaptive weights replace fixed or topology-only weighting in distributed GA-LMB fusion.
\item The core weighting backbone combines posterior covariance, realized link quality, and existence confidence.
\item A weak structure-aware decoupled refinement yields the best consensus performance under heterogeneous packet loss without materially degrading local tracking accuracy.
\end{highlights}

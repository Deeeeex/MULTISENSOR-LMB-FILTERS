\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related-work}

Random finite-set filters provide a Bayesian framework for tracking uncertain
target cardinality and measurement association. PHD filters introduced a
tractable first-moment recursion \cite{Mahler2003PHD}; labeled RFS models add
persistent identities \cite{Vo2014LRFS}; and LMB filters provide a practical
independent-Bernoulli approximation \cite{Reuter2014LMB}. Multi-sensor
GLMB/LMB variants extend this framework to cooperative sensing
\cite{Vo2019MSGLMB}. When cross-correlations are unknown, KLA/GCI fusion
combines densities through a normalized weighted geometric mean
\cite{Battistelli2014KLA}; RFS instances include exponential-mixture PHD and
multi-Bernoulli fusion \cite{Uney2013EMDPHD,Wang2017MBGCI}. We do not change
the local LMB recursion or propose a new KLA rule. We analyze the representation
consumed by one projected Gaussian receiver.

Communication is reduced at several layers. Event-triggered LMB methods change
when full updates are sent \cite{Shen2022EventTriggeredLMB,
Shen2023AdaptiveEventTriggeredLMB,Shen2024IntegralEventLMB}; related triggers
operate on $M\delta$-GLMB labels or hypotheses
\cite{Li2026EventTriggeredMdeltaGLMB}. Partial-consensus PHD fusion selects
Gaussian components \cite{Li2019PartialConsensusGMPHD}, whereas state and
covariance projection sends compact estimates at a common time
\cite{Xue2026StateCovProjection}. Our design is different in origin: the fixed
receiver operator induces the message fields. The resulting equivalence is
conditional on that operator and therefore narrower than general Gaussian-
mixture KLA approximation.
